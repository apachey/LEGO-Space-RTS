This document converts the four Phase 01 faction fantasies into authoritative RTS civilizations.

- Rock Raiders — 18 released source entries;

- Mars Mission — 19 released source entries;

Phase 01 already established the four fundamental strategic identities:

- **Rock Raiders:** infrastructure momentum and physical terrain transformation;
- **Astronauts:** planned adaptability through modular equipment and combined arms;
- **Aliens:** crystal/energy tempo and concentrated offensive power;
- **Martians:** mechanical networks, routing, and battlefield manipulation.

Phase 02 makes those identities mechanically concrete.

Throughout this document:

**OFFICIAL SOURCE MATERIAL** means a verified LEGO set, model, visual language, mechanism, vehicle, structure, character, tool, creature, or other documented source feature.

**GAME ADAPTATION** means an RTS mechanic extrapolated from official source material.

**NEW GAME CONTENT** means an invented system, structure category, infrastructure element, or other functionality required to complete a competitive RTS faction. New content is never represented as an official LEGO product.

# PART I — THE FOUR FACTION FANTASIES

## Extremely Concise Comparison

| Faction | What commanding it fundamentally feels like |
|---|---|
| **Rock Raiders** | Build an industrial operation that physically develops hostile territory until your economy, repairs, defenses, and heavy machinery operate as one worksite. |
| **Astronauts** | Read the situation, configure the expedition for the mission, combine complementary systems, and change your composition more intelligently than the opponent. |
| **Aliens** | Commit crystals to power, create a temporary technological advantage, transform that advantage into rapid pressure, then disengage before the window closes. |
| **Martians** | Build a connected settlement network, route resources and reinforcements through it, and use eccentric machinery to make positioning work differently for you than for the enemy. |

The factions therefore do not correspond to conventional RTS stereotypes such as “slow,” “balanced,” “rush,” and “defensive.”

Their defining verbs are:

**Rock Raiders:** excavate → service → industrialize → advance.

**Astronauts:** survey → configure → combine → refit.

**Aliens:** commit → charge → transform → strike.

**Martians:** connect → route → reposition → redirect.

---

## ROCK RAIDERS — FACTION FANTASY

### Core Player Fantasy

Operate a dangerous industrial expedition whose machines progressively turn hostile geology into productive, defensible territory.

### Strategic Fantasy

Create infrastructure momentum.

A Rock Raider position becomes stronger when extraction, power, hauling, repair, production, and terrain development reinforce one another.

### Economic Fantasy

Extract more value from difficult ground than anyone else, then physically move and process that material through an increasingly efficient industrial operation.

### Construction Fantasy

A base begins as a rough worksite and becomes a dense, interconnected industrial installation filled with cranes, depots, vehicle access, machinery, warning markings, and active construction.

### Battlefield Fantasy

Advance like an engineering operation rather than an armored regiment.

Drilling, demolition, excavation machinery, heavy industrial tools, repair support, and prepared worksites allow Raiders to methodically take and hold ground.

### Visual Fantasy

A heavy mining expedition in motion: dark turquoise machinery, dark industrial frames, hazard-yellow accents, exposed functional tools, drills, loaders, cranes, lights, and visibly mechanical construction.

---

## ASTRONAUTS — FACTION FANTASY

### Core Player Fantasy

Command a sophisticated exploration force that prepares the right equipment for the mission and combines rugged field engineering with high-performance mission systems.

### Strategic Fantasy

Solve changing strategic problems through planned adaptability.

### Economic Fantasy

Operate a dependable expedition economy that can redirect capacity between exploration, mining, mobility, air operations, and military requirements without becoming best-in-class at all of them simultaneously.

### Construction Fantasy

Deploy clean modular expedition infrastructure that becomes functional quickly and can be specialized as the mission changes.

### Battlefield Fantasy

Fight through combined arms.

Ground explorers, modular vehicles, specialized machinery, aerospace systems, reconnaissance, transport, and support work best when composed intelligently rather than massed as one universal unit.

### Visual Fantasy

One human civilization containing two deliberately visible engineering traditions:

- rugged Life on Mars-derived field exploration technology;
- clean white/orange Mars Mission-derived high-performance mission technology.

Neither is visually erased.

---

## ALIENS — FACTION FANTASY

### Core Player Fantasy

Command a technological civilization that turns crystals into operational intensity and turns operational intensity into frighteningly fast pressure.

### Strategic Fantasy

Create windows of local superiority before the opponent can stabilize.

### Economic Fantasy

Continually decide whether valuable crystals should become permanent advanced technology or remain committed to a reusable source of immediate power.

### Construction Fantasy

Establish compact technological footholds by landing, unfolding, docking, and reconfiguring spacecraft-derived infrastructure rather than erecting conventional terrestrial buildings.

### Battlefield Fantasy

Change configurations, redirect pressure, concentrate energy, strike vulnerable locations, and force the opponent to respond on Alien timing.

### Visual Fantasy

Black/lime Mars Mission Alien machinery operating at visibly different energy states: curved hulls, folding sections, glowing crystal systems, transformation, and intense directional energy effects.

---

## MARTIANS — FACTION FANTASY

### Core Player Fantasy

Operate an established eccentric civilization whose machines, settlements, transport routes, and utility systems interact as one enormous mechanism.

### Strategic Fantasy

Gain advantage by controlling relationships between locations.

### Economic Fantasy

Collect resources locally but distribute value through a connected settlement network.

### Construction Fantasy

Extend an inhabited mechanical settlement through platforms, workshops, articulated machinery, stations, and Aero Tube infrastructure.

### Battlefield Fantasy

Move reinforcements through unexpected routes, reposition friendly machinery, interfere with enemy formations, and make supposedly safe or inconvenient locations strategically useful.

### Visual Fantasy

Life on Mars machinery enlarged into a coherent civilization: blue and sand-red forms, translucent neon-green elements, unusual vertical profiles, hover systems, walkers, tubes, pumps, slides, mechanical arms, and deliberately eccentric articulation.

## Seven Primary Asymmetry Axes

### 1. TERRITORIAL INVESTMENT

**Rock Raiders:** territory becomes stronger because they industrialize it.

**Astronauts:** territory becomes useful because they configure it for the current mission.

**Aliens:** territory matters as a temporary launch point for pressure and crystal access.

**Martians:** territory becomes stronger because it connects useful locations.

---

### 2. RESOURCE CONVERSION & LOGISTICS

**Rock Raiders:** extraction → physical hauling → processing → industrial throughput.

**Astronauts:** standardized acquisition → flexible allocation → mission specialization.

**Aliens:** acquisition → crystal decision → permanent technology or temporary Charge.

**Martians:** local acquisition → station deposit → network distribution.

---

### 3. CONSTRUCTION & PRODUCTION ARCHITECTURE

**Rock Raiders:** stable serviced industrial worksites.

**Astronauts:** modular prefabricated expedition architecture.

**Aliens:** spacecraft-derived deployable infrastructure.

**Martians:** mechanical settlement stations and connections.

---

### 4. ADAPTATION VS COMMITMENT

**Rock Raiders:** high commitment; efficiency rewards staying and developing.

**Astronauts:** lowest commitment; they can deliberately reconfigure.

**Aliens:** extreme timing commitment; temporary power must accomplish something.

**Martians:** spatial commitment; network geometry determines available options.

---

### 5. MOBILITY & STRATEGIC REACH

**Rock Raiders:** movement is strongest inside their developed operating space.

**Astronauts:** broadly mobile across the whole map.

**Aliens:** rapidly redirect offensive threat.

**Martians:** disproportionately mobile between prepared locations.

---

### 6. BATTLEFIELD CONTROL & DAMAGE TIMING

**Rock Raiders:** sustained pressure and industrial breaching.

**Astronauts:** composition-driven combined arms.

**Aliens:** concentrated bursts and attack windows.

**Martians:** positional distortion and reinforcement geometry.

---

### 7. RECOVERY & ESCALATION

**Rock Raiders:** repair and surviving worksites restore industrial momentum.

**Astronauts:** rebuild into a different composition.

**Aliens:** preserve or reacquire crystal power to generate another window.

**Martians:** reroute around damaged sections of the network.

# PART III — ROCK RAIDERS

## Source-Control Statement

**OFFICIAL SOURCE MATERIAL:** Rock Raiders provides scouts, drills, loaders, heavy mining vehicles, transport, HQ infrastructure, specialist crews, tools, crystal/resource material, and Rock Monsters.

**GAME ADAPTATION:** the faction converts those mining and infrastructure motifs into an RTS-wide industrial logistics and terrain system.

**NEW GAME CONTENT:** additional production, defensive, anti-air, and logistical infrastructure may be created where the official sets cannot by themselves provide a complete competitive faction. Such assets must look like Rock Raiders industrial equipment, not military sci-fi.

---

## 1. Player Fantasy

Minute-to-minute Raider play alternates between operating the worksite and advancing its perimeter.

The player continually asks:

- Where is the next useful geological position?
- Can the worksite support another operation?
- Which path should be excavated?
- Is hauling becoming inefficient?
- Where should repair and heavy production move next?
- Can the army advance while remaining supported by infrastructure?

The faction rewards a player who enjoys making territory progressively work better.

---

## 2. Economy

### Ore / Metal

Rock Raiders have the strongest natural relationship with Ore/Metal.

Their advantage is not a passive percentage bonus.

They can exploit deposits that require more excavation, move larger quantities efficiently once a worksite is established, and improve extraction throughput by developing infrastructure around a mining operation.

### Energy

Energy is the operational backbone of heavy Raider industry.

Advanced facilities, repair systems, large processing equipment, and defensive machinery place increasing demand on the worksite's Energy supply.

Energy pressure therefore grows with industrial success.

A Raider cannot build an enormous advanced base while treating Energy as irrelevant.

### Crystals

Crystals are valuable high-grade mining targets.

Rock Raiders extract them efficiently but do not build their entire macro cycle around them.

They primarily support advanced technology, specialized energy systems, and high-end machinery.

### Harvesting

Resource gathering consists of three visible stages:

**extract → haul → process.**

Workers and machines mine deposits.

Automated haulers transport extracted material to the nearest functional worksite receiving point.

Processing infrastructure converts the material into usable strategic resources.

### The Worksite Network — PRIMARY UNIQUE MACRO MECHANIC

**GAME ADAPTATION.**

A Rock Raider base is organized into **Worksites**.

An HQ or service/depot node creates a serviced industrial area. Nearby Raider buildings, processing equipment, repair systems, production facilities, and resource receiving points can belong to that Worksite.

Connected Worksites create the **Worksite Network**.

Being serviced improves:

- resource routing;
- repair access;
- construction support;
- heavy production logistics;
- operational resilience.

The player manages the placement of service nodes and the geography of industrial operations.

The player does **not** manually order every Ore shipment.

Physical hauling remains visible, but routine routing is automatic.

A disconnected building does not instantly shut down. It continues functioning at reduced logistical efficiency so a single node loss cannot create an unfair binary collapse.

### Storage

Storage is physically represented through industrial depots, containers, loading areas, crystal handling, and processing structures.

Destroying resource infrastructure can disrupt throughput or expose stored material without requiring every individual resource piece to be simulated as a competitive object.

### Economic Scaling

Rock Raider economic scaling is **capital-intensive**.

A new mining site initially costs more attention and infrastructure than an Astronaut forward outpost.

Once properly serviced, it becomes extremely efficient.

The faction therefore possesses a pronounced curve:

**slower establishment → strong mature operation.**

### Economic Vulnerabilities

- exposed haulers between extraction and serviced areas;
- overloaded or poorly positioned service nodes;
- Energy infrastructure;
- expensive undeveloped expansions;
- loss of processing capacity;
- attacks that force heavy machines to leave productive work.

---

## 3. Construction

Raider structures are built physically.

Crew and construction machinery deliver components, position foundations, lock modules together, and install industrial systems.

Construction is faster and more reliable inside a serviced Worksite.

Remote construction remains possible but is deliberately less efficient.

### Base Organization

A mature Raider base favors:

- resource-facing industrial zones;
- processing near hauling routes;
- central service access;
- vehicle circulation;
- repair infrastructure;
- heavy production with sufficient operating space;
- reinforced defensive edges.

The base should visually read as an operating mine rather than a collection of unrelated structures.

### Production Zones

Large industrial production gains practical value from being placed within established service infrastructure.

This creates reasons to defend industrial centers without forcing all production into one structure.

### Defensive Infrastructure

Raider defenses resemble:

- mining safety systems adapted to conflict;
- reinforced industrial barriers;
- heavy tools;
- drilling/demolition machinery;
- powered worksite installations.

They do not resemble modern turrets mounted on military bunkers.

---

## 4. Terrain Interaction

### Excavation — SECONDARY STRATEGIC MECHANIC

**GAME ADAPTATION derived from Rock Raiders drilling, mining, and underground identity.**

Competitive maps contain clearly authored **Excavatable Terrain Features**.

Examples include:

- weak rock walls;
- rubble blockages;
- mineable ridges;
- collapsed tunnel mouths;
- resource-bearing rock faces.

Rock Raiders can order appropriate machinery to excavate them.

Excavation may:

- open an optional attack route;
- shorten a hauling route;
- expose a secondary resource pocket;
- create a flank;
- remove an obstacle from an industrial area.

It does **not** permit arbitrary voxel excavation of the entire competitive map.

### Universal Topology Rule

No essential expansion, victory objective, or mandatory strategic resource is accessible only to Rock Raiders.

Once Raiders open a route, other ground factions may use it unless a normal battlefield obstacle independently prevents them.

### Counterplay

Opponents can:

- scout excavation activity;
- attack drilling machinery;
- contest the future exit;
- use the newly opened route themselves;
- attack the industrial operation that justified the excavation;
- prepare defenses around a predictable excavation feature.

Rock Raider map interaction therefore creates new strategic decisions for both players.

---

## 5. Expansion

A Raider expansion is an **industrial project**.

The faction is incentivized to expand toward:

- rich geology;
- clustered resource deposits;
- terrain that can be profitably developed;
- defensible industrial space.

A new position usually progresses through:

**survey → establish service → extract → process → reinforce.**

A Raider can take a quick resource outpost, but its defining strength appears only after investment.

---

## 6. Production

Producing Raider machines should feel like commissioning industrial equipment.

Production structures contain:

- assembly bays;
- lifting gear;
- exposed machinery;
- tool racks;
- vehicle ramps;
- moving cranes.

Heavy production benefits strongly from stable serviced infrastructure.

Raider reinforcement is therefore powerful when fighting near established worksites and slower when continually operating at extreme distance.

---

## 7. Logistics

Logistics matters but does not become a hauling-management simulation.

The player makes **strategic** logistical decisions:

- where service nodes go;
- where processing occurs;
- how far resources travel;
- whether a forward operation is worth developing;
- whether a heavy army can remain supported.

Routine transport is automated.

### Industrial Recovery

Damaged Raider machinery repairs particularly effectively near service infrastructure.

Wreckage and salvage concepts may later support additional recovery mechanics, but Phase 03 must not assume a universal resurrection system.

---

## 8. Scouting

Rock Raider reconnaissance is primarily **industrial reconnaissance**.

They are excellent at discovering:

- resource density;
- excavatable features;
- geological hazards;
- alternative routes;
- useful worksite locations.

Their scouts remain fully capable of detecting enemy forces, but the faction does not receive the broadest long-range battlefield information package.

A Raider scout answers:

**“What can we do with this terrain?”**

more readily than:

**“What exact army composition is hidden three screens away?”**

---

## 9. Combat

### Army Philosophy

A Rock Raider army resembles a protected engineering convoy.

Combat machines should usually remain understandable as:

- drills;
- loaders;
- cutters;
- demolition equipment;
- excavators;
- heavy utility vehicles;
- industrial transports;
- engineering platforms.

### Engagement Style

Raiders prefer:

- deliberate advances;
- repair-supported fighting;
- favorable chokepoints;
- forward servicing;
- sustained pressure;
- forcing opponents away from valuable terrain.

### Range Profile

The faction leans toward short and medium range.

Long-range systems exist where required for a complete RTS but should visually resemble industrial projection, drilling, demolition, launching, or heavy engineering technology.

### Durability

High.

Durability supports sustained industrial operation.

It does not grant Raiders permission to ignore positioning or counters.

### Mobility

Tactical movement can be respectable.

Strategic redeployment of the entire heavy force is comparatively costly.

Transport assets and prepared infrastructure compensate for this limitation.

### Siege

Rock Raiders are naturally strong at siege.

Their siege identity comes from:

- heavy drilling;
- demolition;
- cutting;
- breaching;
- industrial pressure against structures.

### Support

Key support themes:

- repair;
- recovery;
- hauling;
- detection of geological opportunities;
- demolition preparation;
- worksite servicing.

### Defensive Warfare

Their strongest defensive state is not a ring of generic guns.

It is an industrial area in which:

- defenders repair efficiently;
- routes are understood;
- reinforcement is short;
- heavy machinery has operating room;
- attackers must pass prepared industrial obstacles.

### Offensive Pressure

The defining Raider offensive is the **Industrial Advance**.

A force takes ground, establishes limited forward service, repairs, brings up heavier machinery, and continues.

It should feel like a mining operation eating into hostile territory.

---

## 10. Technology Progression

Raider advancement means increasing **industrial depth**.

Early advancement improves:

- surveying;
- basic excavation;
- hauling;
- worksite establishment.

Mid-game advancement introduces:

- stronger processing;
- advanced repair;
- heavy machinery;
- improved terrain operation;
- better defensive engineering.

Late-game advancement integrates:

- heavy transport;
- very large machinery;
- advanced drilling;
- high-output Energy systems;
- major industrial siege capability.

No Raider technology tier suddenly transforms the faction into a futuristic military force.

---

## 11. Structural Strengths

- strongest mature economic throughput;
- strongest sustained terrain interaction;
- excellent prepared defense;
- strong structure siege;
- strong repair and recovery near infrastructure;
- efficient fighting around developed territory;
- good ability to convert a resource position into a permanent stronghold.

---

## 12. Structural Weaknesses

- expansion strength arrives after investment rather than immediately;
- poor strategic decisions create expensive infrastructure in the wrong locations;
- heavy forces do not redirect across the map effortlessly;
- logistics and Energy infrastructure create real harassment targets;
- specialized tools can create awkward compositions if the player scouts poorly;
- fighting continuously beyond service reach erodes the faction's efficiency.

---

## 13. Counterplay

| Raider Advantage | Opponent Response |
|---|---|
| Mature Worksite efficiency | Attack expansions before full development or force investment in multiple distant fronts. |
| Repair-supported armies | Deny service infrastructure, disengage rather than feed inefficient fights, or use siege to force repair systems backward. |
| Excavated routes | Scout drilling, contest exits, or use the opened route yourself. |
| Durable machinery | Use appropriate anti-heavy tools, mobility, isolation, or concentrated damage instead of inefficiently attacking the toughest target first. |
| Strong siege | Attack support units, flank slow siege elements, or force the Raider army to redeploy. |
| Industrial defense | Control more of the map and force the Raiders to leave the prepared area. |
| Economic scaling | Harass hauling, Energy, processing, and undeveloped expansions. |

The architecture deliberately leaves room for:

# PART IV — ASTRONAUTS

## Source-Control Statement

Life on Mars human material provides rugged rovers, unconventional wheels, modular vehicle sections, compact craft, and solar-oriented exploration technology. Mars Mission humans provide white/orange mission hardware, mining machines, command infrastructure, dropships, fighters, robots, transformable vehicles, and highly modular craft.

**GAME ADAPTATION:** these become two complementary engineering traditions within one faction.

**NEW GAME CONTENT:** shared command, service, and modular interface infrastructure is allowed to unify them mechanically without visually blending their original engineering languages.

---

# ASTRONAUT TECHNOLOGY UNIFICATION — CANONICAL SOLUTION

The Astronaut faction contains two parallel engineering traditions that specialize by **operational philosophy**, not by technological age.

## FIELD SYSTEMS — LIFE ON MARS-DERIVED

Field Systems emphasize:

- rugged exploration;
- surface mobility;
- endurance;
- surveying;
- solar/remote operation;
- unconventional but practical vehicle arrangements;
- modular physical payloads;
- sustaining operations far from a main base.

They are not “early technology.”

Advanced Field Systems remain strategically relevant in late-game armies and infrastructure.

## MISSION SYSTEMS — MARS MISSION-DERIVED

Mission Systems emphasize:

- high-performance aerospace;
- specialized extraction;
- command infrastructure;
- high-output mission vehicles;
- transformation;
- rapid deployment;
- purpose-built combat and support configurations.

They are not “the upgrade that replaces Life on Mars.”

## Canonical Relationship

**Field Systems keep the expedition operating.**

**Mission Systems let the expedition perform specialized high-intensity tasks.**

A normal competitive match should regularly display both.

The faction is strongest when these traditions support each other.

---

## 1. Player Fantasy

The Astronaut player is a mission commander.

Minute-to-minute decisions revolve around:

- reading enemy intent;
- choosing the right composition;
- positioning mobile assets;
- deciding when existing equipment is worth refitting;
- deciding whether a forward expedition needs reconnaissance, logistics, extraction, air support, or combat capability.

---

## 2. Economy

Astronauts possess the most conventional and readable economy of the four factions.

That does not mean it is mechanically empty.

Its strength is **reallocation**.

### Ore / Metal

Reliable construction resource.

Astronaut extraction does not match the Raider ceiling in deeply industrialized territory, but it requires less setup.

### Energy

Particularly important to:

- advanced mission systems;
- air operations;
- refitting;
- high-performance technology.

Field Systems reduce the difficulty of sustaining remote operations, including through solar-derived source motifs.

### Crystals

Used selectively for:

- advanced mission packages;
- specialized propulsion;
- high-performance technology;
- upper-tier research.

Astronauts should be able to pursue several viable compositions without immediately requiring massive crystal expenditure.

---

## 3. Resource Use

The faction's resource identity is **flexible allocation**.

A player who invests heavily in aerospace has a different Energy profile from one building a durable ground expedition.

A player who pursues advanced mission technology consumes more Crystals than one expanding broadly with simpler Field Systems.

The economy therefore responds to strategy.

---

## 4. Construction

Astronaut structures arrive as standardized components.

Workers, rovers, or construction systems:

- deploy foundations;
- place prefabricated modules;
- connect power/data systems;
- install mission hardware.

Construction is comparatively quick and clean.

There is no Raider-style requirement to industrialize the surrounding terrain.

---

## 5. Base Organization

Astronaut bases are organized around **Expedition Hubs** and specialized modules.

The faction does not require a strict adjacency puzzle.

Instead, base organization emphasizes:

- service access;
- transport routes;
- aircraft operating space;
- modular expansion;
- defensible but flexible layouts.

---

## 6. Expansion

Astronauts establish useful expansions quickly.

An outpost should be able to begin basic operation without extensive prerequisite infrastructure.

Its role can then change.

A mining outpost may become:

- reconnaissance support;
- logistics staging;
- air-support territory;
- production support.

Changing purpose costs time and resources.

---

## 7. Production

Production is organized around standardized vehicle families and mission-specific configurations.

Not every Astronaut asset is modular.

The roster must instead contain a curated subset of assets whose configurations create meaningful strategic decisions.

This prevents the faction becoming a screen of dozens of small toggles.

---

## 8. Modularity

### Mission Refit — PRIMARY UNIQUE MACRO MECHANIC

**GAME ADAPTATION based on Life on Mars modular vehicle sections and Mars Mission transformation/detachable mission-system motifs.**

Selected Astronaut units and structures possess a limited set of **Mission Configurations**.

A compatible asset may be refitted at an appropriate Service Hub.

Refitting:

- takes time;
- has a resource cost;
- cannot normally occur during combat;
- cannot freely erase a scouting mistake;
- is visually represented by changing physical modules.

A player can therefore transform existing investment into a different strategic answer, but only by anticipating the change early enough.

### Design Rule

Most configurable assets receive a small number of strongly differentiated configurations.

They do not receive generic statistical sliders.

---

## 9. Logistics

Astronaut logistics is intentionally moderate.

Resources do not require Raider-level hauling infrastructure.

Armies do not receive Martian network teleportation.

Instead, their logistical identity comes from:

- transports;
- mobile service;
- forward hubs;
- airlift;
- modular field systems.

### Forward Service Network — SECONDARY STRATEGIC MECHANIC

Mission refitting and full field servicing require appropriate Astronaut service infrastructure.

Forward Service Hubs therefore extend the area in which an Astronaut army can efficiently:

- repair;
- refit;
- reload/recover specialized systems;
- alter operational composition.

This makes expansion and forward basing strategically meaningful without creating a second resource-routing minigame.

---

## 10. Scouting

Astronauts possess the strongest general-purpose reconnaissance package.

Potential information methods include:

- ground scouts;
- aircraft;
- radar;
- scanners;
- forward probes;
- mission configuration options.

Their information strength is **breadth**.

Unlike Raiders, they do not specialize primarily in geology.

Unlike Aliens, their information does not depend chiefly on aggressive incursions.

Unlike Martians, it does not depend strongly on already-established territory.

---

## 11. Mobility

Astronauts have the most broadly reliable mobility.

They possess tools for:

- ground maneuver;
- rough-terrain travel;
- transport;
- aerospace movement;
- rapid response.

They are not automatically the fastest faction in every circumstance.

Aliens remain better at explosive threat redirection.

Martians remain better at prepared network transfer.

---

## 12. Air Capability

Astronauts possess the broadest conventional air capability.

Air assets support:

- reconnaissance;
- interception;
- transport;
- harassment;
- combined-arms combat.

Air power requires appropriate Energy, infrastructure, and support.

It cannot replace ground map control.

Important objectives, durable bases, resource extraction, and most territorial ownership continue to require surface presence.

---

## 13. Combined-Arms Philosophy

Astronaut strength is multiplicative rather than universal.

A scouting element identifies the problem.

A ground line establishes contact.

A specialized weapon solves the immediate target.

Air or transport changes positioning.

Support maintains the engagement.

No individual component should provide the entire answer efficiently.

---

## 14. Combat

### Range Profile

Broadest conventional range distribution.

### Durability

Medium and dependable.

Field-derived units may favor endurance.

High-performance Mission systems may trade some durability for specialized capability.

### Damage

Accurate, composition-dependent, conventional by the standards of the setting.

### Siege

Reliable but specialized.

### Harassment

Flexible.

Astronauts can harass through:

- air;
- light vehicles;
- transport;
- range;
- temporary composition changes.

### Battlefield Behavior — COMBINED-ARMS RESPONSE

The defining Astronaut behavior is to identify the opponent's current plan, bring the correct group of complementary tools, and change that group when the strategic problem changes.

Their advantage is **having access to answers**.

Their skill test is **choosing the correct answers early enough**.

---

## 15. Technology Progression

Astronaut advancement is a branching expansion of **mission capability**.

Field Systems and Mission Systems remain parallel.

Progression unlocks:

- more specialized configurations;
- stronger service infrastructure;
- improved transport;
- advanced air operations;
- sophisticated extraction;
- higher-performance combined arms.

Late technology increasingly allows cross-line cooperation.

It does not make one lineage obsolete.

---

## 16. Structural Strengths

- broadest viable composition space;
- excellent scouting;
- reliable mobility;
- strong air integration;
- ability to refit existing investment;
- quickly useful expansions;
- intuitive traditional RTS fundamentals;
- strong capacity to respond to unusual enemy strategies.

---

## 17. Structural Weaknesses

- individually specialized factions outperform Astronauts in their signature domains;
- refitting consumes time and resources;
- refitting requires service access;
- spreading across too many technology options creates inefficient spending;
- a poorly composed force remains punishable even though another answer exists somewhere in the roster;
- high mobility and air capability place meaningful pressure on Energy and infrastructure.

---

## 18. Counterplay

| Astronaut Advantage | Opponent Response |
|---|---|
| Broad answers | Force multiple simultaneous problems so one composition cannot solve everything. |
| Refit | Attack during refit downtime, deny Service Hubs, or change composition again after the Astronaut commits. |
| Strong scouting | Protect key information, destroy scouts, use timing changes rather than hidden rule interactions. |
| Air mobility | Attack service infrastructure, use anti-air coverage, threaten ground objectives that aircraft cannot capture or hold alone. |
| Fast useful expansion | Pressure outposts before they are specialized and defended. |
| Combined arms | Disrupt one necessary component rather than fighting the whole formation on ideal terms. |

---

- insignia;
- UI;
- crew language;
- mission markings;
- standardized status lights;
- shared base connection standards.

They do **not** share one homogenized vehicle silhouette.

### Mars Mission-Derived Systems

The branch therefore favors:

- white/light-gray structural pieces;

- exposed Technic/mechanical connections;

### Silhouette

Field Systems:

- rugged;

- exploratory;
- functionally unconventional.

Mission Systems:

- cleaner;

### Architecture

### Effects

### Construction

# PART V — ALIENS

## Source-Control Statement

Mars Mission provides dedicated Alien jets, ETX craft, transforming machines, black/lime attack vehicles, Alien Infiltrator technology, and the Alien Mothership. The source range contains substantially fewer conventional base/economic structures than a complete RTS faction requires.

Consequently, Alien infrastructure requires the largest controlled amount of NEW GAME CONTENT.

---

## 1. Player Fantasy

The player operates a technologically concentrated offensive civilization.

Minute-to-minute Alien decisions ask:

- How many crystals should remain available for permanent technology?
- How many should be committed to Charge?
- Is this the correct moment to surge?
- Which flank can be threatened?
- Which machine should change configuration?
- When should pressure stop before attrition becomes unfavorable?

---

## 2. Economy

Alien baseline economy remains complete and functional.

### Ore / Metal

Builds:

- physical hulls;
- structures;
- machinery;
- basic production.

Aliens cannot replace Ore entirely with crystals.

### Energy

Runs normal technological operation.

Energy is required before Crystal Charge can create exceptional output.

### Crystals

Crystals have **two competing Alien uses**:

1. permanent expenditure on advanced technology and high-grade assets;
2. reversible commitment to the Crystal Charge system.

This dual purpose is the core of Alien macro.

---

## 3. Crystal Economy

# Crystal Charge — PRIMARY UNIQUE MACRO MECHANIC

**GAME ADAPTATION** from the strong Mars Mission Alien/crystal relationship.

**Resonance Core infrastructure is NEW GAME CONTENT.**

### Resonance Cores

Alien bases contain **Resonance Cores**: visible black/lime technological installations derived aesthetically from Alien craft hulls, crystal containment, and Mothership/ETX machinery.

A player may commit stored Crystals into Core resonance slots.

A committed Crystal:

- is temporarily unavailable as normal currency;
- increases maximum Charge;
- increases Charge generation.

### Charge

**Charge is a derived faction resource, not a fourth strategic map resource.**

It cannot be mined directly.

Charge is generated only through the Alien Crystal system.

Selected Alien systems consume Charge for:

- high-output transformation states;
- temporary production acceleration;
- rapid mobility;
- advanced weapon operation;
- specific faction-level pressure tools.

Basic Alien units remain functional without constant Charge consumption.

The player is therefore not forced to click abilities continuously merely to make the army work.

### Strategic Tradeoff

A Crystal cannot simultaneously be:

- committed for immediate Charge;
- and permanently spent on technology.

The Alien player continually chooses between:

**power now** and **capability later**.

### Withdrawal

Committed Crystals can be withdrawn, but extraction is telegraphed and takes time.

Charge capacity drops as commitment ends.

### Core Destruction

Destroying or disabling a Core interrupts the Charge economy.

Committed Crystals do not instantly become free resources for the attacker.

They become recoverable/salvageable material around the destroyed system or enter an equivalent delayed recovery state defined by later economy tuning.

The canonical requirement is that attacking a committed Core creates real risk without producing an uncontrollable one-click economic transfer.

---

## 4. Harvesting

Alien harvest systems prioritize:

- low setup time;
- compact footprint;
- rapid access to contested Crystals.

They do not outperform fully developed Rock Raider industrial extraction over long periods.

A defining Alien economic question is not only:

**“How much can I mine?”**

but:

**“How quickly can this resource become pressure?”**

---

## 5. Construction

### Deployable Craft Infrastructure

**NEW GAME CONTENT.**

Alien structures are designed as credible stationary or deployable descendants of Mars Mission Alien spacecraft.

Construction commonly follows:

**arrive → anchor → unfold → connect → energize.**

Structures may resemble:

- landed hull sections;
- unfolded ETX frames;
- docked spacecraft modules;
- Mothership-derived bays;
- crystal containment devices.

Craft-derived infrastructure combines armored hulls with skeletal supports,
living conduits and bio-organic systems, subject to source/adaptation review
(Phase 02A). Generic organic hives, unrelated tentacles, insect nests, gothic
monoliths and arbitrary alien towers are not substitutes for that technology.

---

## 6. Base Organization

Alien bases are compact technological clusters.

Important spatial concerns include:

- protecting Resonance Cores;
- maintaining production access;
- creating launch lanes;
- defending crystal operations;
- preserving room for deploying/reconfiguring infrastructure.

They should look closer to a fleet operating from landed technological hardpoints than to a terrestrial city.

---

## 7. Expansion

Alien expansions are **footholds**.

They become useful quickly but are less naturally durable than Raider industrial expansions.

High-value locations include:

- crystals;
- useful attack approaches;
- flanking geography;
- staging positions.

A greedy Alien expansion should visibly signal the intent to increase future Charge or attack reach.

---

## 8. Production

Alien production is responsive to Charge but not dependent on permanently running an “overdrive” toggle.

Charge can provide limited temporary production acceleration.

Doing so consumes the same derived resource needed for battlefield pressure.

The player therefore cannot maximize production and combat intensity at once without substantial crystal commitment.

---

## 9. Mobility

Aliens possess the strongest offensive redirection.

Movement identity includes:

- fast craft;
- hover/flight;
- compact machines;
- transforming mobility modes.

Mobility should create uncertainty about **where** the attack arrives, not uncertainty about game rules.

---

## 10. Scouting

Alien scouting is aggressive.

Their best reconnaissance tools prefer to:

- enter enemy territory;
- observe briefly;
- force a response;
- escape.

They have comparatively less emphasis on persistent safe surveillance.

Enemy information is often acquired as a by-product of harassment.

---

## 11. Transformation / Reconfiguration

### Deploy / Reconfigure — SECONDARY STRATEGIC MECHANIC

Official Mars Mission Alien material directly supports transforming and reconfiguring craft.

A curated subset of Alien assets therefore switch between clearly different operational states.

Common design relationships include:

- movement ↔ firepower;
- compact flight ↔ ground combat;
- mobile craft ↔ deployed support;
- pursuit ↔ siege.

Transformations are:

- visually obvious;
- mechanically meaningful;
- constrained by transition time;
- readable to the opponent.

The entire faction does not transform constantly.

---

## 12. Harassment

Harassment is central.

Alien aggression emerges from system interaction:

**fast expansion or harvesting → crystal commitment → Charge generation → mobility/transformation → temporary pressure.**

Units are not simply made artificially cheap.

Successful Alien harassment:

- kills workers;
- interrupts Energy;
- damages production;
- forces defensive positioning;
- denies crystals;
- causes the opponent to spend resources before they intended.

---

## 13. Combat

### Army Philosophy

Alien armies use sharp, high-output biomechanical technology; their strategic
identity is energy tempo, not cheap biological swarm production.

### Engagement Style

- approach from unexpected direction;
- create favorable local numbers;
- trigger high-output states;
- destroy a valuable target;
- leave before attrition becomes inefficient.

### Durability

Moderate-low as a faction average.

Some major Alien craft can be substantial, but the faction should not excel at passive damage absorption.

### Damage

Burst-oriented during Charge windows.

Baseline damage remains functional.

### Range

Mixed, with several units trading movement for stronger firing configurations.

### Siege

Alien siege is dangerous but telegraphed.

A mobile craft may deploy into a stronger attack state.

The opponent receives an opportunity to:

- attack during deployment;
- flank it;
- force relocation.

### Defense

Alien defense is active.

Instead of building the strongest fortifications, they:

- intercept;
- counterattack;
- reposition;
- threaten the enemy's own economy.

### Support

Alien support emphasizes:

- Charge distribution;
- disruption;
- scanning;
- transformation assistance;
- containment/capture-inspired technology.

Permanent loss-of-control mechanics are not a defining faction feature.

### Defining Battlefield Behavior — SURGE WINDOWS

Alien armies become temporarily more dangerous when the player commits Charge to an engagement.

The window is:

- observable;
- finite;
- costly;
- exploitable by an opponent who survives it.

---

## 14. Technology

Alien progression represents increasing **energy intensity**.

Early:

- basic mobility;
- compact harvesting;
- low-scale Charge.

Mid:

- more sophisticated reconfiguration;
- higher Charge capacity;
- stronger disruption;
- improved attack mobility.

Late:

- major ETX-scale technology;
- advanced crystal systems;
- Mothership-derived strategic presence;
- powerful but expensive high-output transformations.

Permanent technological advancement consumes Crystals that might otherwise support Charge.

The faction's core macro dilemma therefore remains relevant for the whole match.

---

## 15. Structural Strengths

- strongest natural offensive tempo;
- powerful harassment;
- rapid threat redirection;
- high local burst potential;
- transformations produce unusual tactical options;
- Crystals can create immediate strategic value rather than only unlock future technology;
- compact expansions become threatening quickly.

---

## 16. Structural Weaknesses

- prolonged attrition is inefficient;
- significant Crystal commitments are visible strategic investments;
- failed surge windows create vulnerability;
- passive defensive play wastes faction strengths;
- Resonance infrastructure creates high-value targets;
- expensive transformed machines can be punished if trapped in the wrong configuration.

---

## 17. Counterplay

| Alien Advantage | Opponent Response |
|---|---|
| Charge surge | Disengage, delay, defend efficiently, then counterattack after Charge is spent. |
| Fast harassment | Layer detection and defense around high-value economy rather than chasing every craft across the map. |
| Crystal commitment | Contest crystal fields or attack Resonance Cores. |
| Transformation | Attack during transition or force the unit to remain in an unfavorable state. |
| Threat redirection | Maintain scouting and avoid concentrating every defense in one location. |
| Production acceleration | Recognize high Charge expenditure and exploit reduced immediate combat reserve. |
| Deployed siege | Flank or force movement. |

---

## 18. Visual Identity

### Primary Colors

- black;
- bright lime.

### Secondary

- dark mechanical gray;
- translucent neon-green crystal/energy elements;
- limited neutral structural colors where required.

The established project registry and Phase 01 identify black/lime construction, curved attack craft, transforming machinery, and Mothership technology as core Mars Mission Alien source language.

### Silhouette

- compact;
- swept;
- curved;
- forward-pointing;
- claw-like and biomechanical where supported by official evidence;
- strong central energy/crystal components.

### Proportions

Machines often appear to wrap around:

- a cockpit;
- a crystal core;
- a central energy mechanism.

### Architecture

Buildings resemble docked or unfolded spacecraft.

Armored exteriors can enclose skeletal supports and living technology. New
working/production structures inherit that compound language; contemporary
human robotic hardware is not an adequate Alien identity. Phase 02A governs
source claims, anatomy and adaptation review across every asset.

### Mechanical Animation

- hull separation;
- wing folding;
- limbs emerging from craft;
- rotating energy assemblies;
- crystal locking;
- transformation.

### Lighting

Charge state is immediately visible.

Higher Charge operation produces brighter but disciplined lime illumination.

### Effects

- resonant pulses;
- focused energy arcs;
- directional lime glow;
- controlled distortion.

Living internal motion may express source-grounded biomechanical function.
No slime/spore weapons, insect-swarm effects or realistic gore are introduced.

### Construction

A craft descends or moves into place and unfolds into infrastructure.

### Destruction

Energy state destabilizes, panels unlock, and the craft-derived structure breaks apart along its transformation seams.

### UI Flavor

Crystal resonance diagrams, angular charge meters, transformation-state icons, strong black/lime contrast.

---

## 19. Audio Identity

**Craft:** smooth alien propulsion, magnetic movement and resonant systems;
bio-organic drives may receive a restrained organic undertone as a reviewed
presentation adaptation, not an unverified literal source sound.

**Transformation:** connected hull folding, locking snaps and conduit flex;
biomechanical systems remain physically connected through the transition.

**Crystal systems:** resonant tonal hum rising in pitch as Charge increases.

**Weapons:** concentrated energy pulses and harmonic discharges. Biomechanical
identity does not replace approved weapons with creature roars or biological attacks.

**Interface:** short crystalline confirmation tones, energetic pulses, clear warning resonance when Charge is low or a Core is attacked.

---

## 20. Source Capacity

The architecture can incorporate:

- small Alien jets as light pressure/scouting sources;
- ETX transforming craft as major transformation references;
- Mothership material as advanced strategic/production inspiration;
- Alien sub-builds from mixed Mars Mission sets;
- containment equipment as disruption/support inspiration;
- Commander/figure-only material as crew, UI, campaign, or production identity;
- bundles without inventing duplicate designs.

The largest likely source gap remains non-combat infrastructure.

That gap is intentionally filled through craft-derived NEW GAME CONTENT rather than generic alien architecture.

# PART VI — MARTIANS

## Source-Control Statement

Life on Mars Martians provide hover craft, Worker Robots, Red Planet vehicles, transforming machinery, walkers, excavation equipment, and most importantly the Aero Tube Hangar with pneumatic tube/hypersled infrastructure.

**GAME ADAPTATION:** the Aero Tube concept becomes the Martian faction's defining macro-logistics system.

**NEW GAME CONTENT:** additional station, workshop, defense, and routing infrastructure is allowed when built from the same platform/tube/articulated mechanical language.

---

## 1. Player Fantasy

Command a civilization that already knows how to live here.

Martian bases are not improvised landing camps.

They feel like pieces of a broader technological culture.

Minute-to-minute Martian play asks:

- Which settlements should be connected?
- Where is network redundancy needed?
- Which units can be moved through the network?
- Can a threatened front be reinforced faster than the opponent expects?
- Can machinery alter the shape of this engagement?
- Can an enemy formation be made inefficient without simply out-damaging it?

---

## 2. Economy

Martian economy is **distributed**.

Extraction occurs at multiple local sites.

Connected settlement infrastructure allows those local economies to act as one.

---

## 3. Resource Use

### Ore / Metal

Collected by mechanical workers and excavation equipment.

Important for:

- machines;
- stations;
- structural expansion.

### Energy

Energy is produced locally but can be distributed through connected settlement infrastructure.

Connected areas share operational strength more efficiently.

Disconnected sites retain local function.

### Crystals

Crystals support:

- advanced mechanisms;
- sophisticated walkers;
- specialized control systems;
- upper-tier technology.

They do not determine the Martian economy in the way they determine Alien Charge.

---

## 4. Construction

Martian construction looks like an established engineering culture extending itself.

Worker machinery:

- assembles platforms;
- installs tubes;
- rotates machinery into position;
- locks articulated modules together.

Buildings appear less standardized than Astronaut structures but more civic and permanent than Alien footholds.

---

## 5. Established-Settlement Identity

Martian architecture may imply off-map civilization through:

- utility junctions;
- maintenance equipment;
- habitation-like mechanical modules;
- transport stations;
- civic machinery;
- local workshops.

Even a military expansion should look as though Martians could actually live and work there.

The faction must never look like a temporary invasion fleet.

---

## 6. Expansion

A new Martian outpost is useful immediately.

Connection makes it better.

Expansion therefore has two stages:

**establish locally → integrate strategically.**

Martians are encouraged to create:

- chains;
- triangles;
- redundant routes;
- distributed clusters.

A single giant central base is strategically possible but wastes the faction's best infrastructure mechanic.

---

## 7. Production

Production is distributed across mechanical workshops.

The Aero Tube system allows eligible produced assets or crew to move efficiently toward another connected front.

Large walkers and heavy vehicles normally leave production facilities conventionally.

This distinction prevents the network from becoming universal army teleportation.

---

# 8. AERO TUBE / LOGISTICS SYSTEM

## Aero Tube Network — PRIMARY UNIQUE MACRO MECHANIC

**GAME ADAPTATION directly motivated by official Aero Tube Hangar and hypersled material.**

### Stations

Major Martian settlement nodes may function as **Aero Stations**.

The player connects compatible Stations with **Tube Links**.

The player chooses connections between nodes.

The system generates the route.

The player does not manually draw every tube corner.

### Resource Routing

Resources deposited at a connected Station become available to the connected settlement network.

Animated cargo/hypersled movement communicates routing without requiring cargo micro.

### Unit Transfer

A defined class of:

- crew;
- small machines;
- selected light/utility vehicles;

can enter an Aero Station and transfer to another connected Station.

Heavy walkers, large vehicles, siege machines, and all arbitrary army assets do not automatically fit.

### Travel

Transfer is not instantaneous.

Movement through the system has:

- visible travel feedback;
- a meaningful but short transit period;
- clear entry and exit points.

### Disruption

Opponents may:

- destroy Stations;
- attack Tube Links;
- camp exits;
- force network rerouting.

### Failure Behavior

Destroying a link does not automatically kill everything traveling through it.

Passengers/cargo are redirected, returned, or safely expelled according to the eventual implementation.

The strategic penalty is:

- delay;
- isolation;
- loss of efficiency;

not arbitrary catastrophic unit deletion.

### Disconnected Settlements

Disconnected Martian bases remain functional.

They use local stored resources and local Energy.

They lose:

- network distribution;
- rapid transfer;
- some operational efficiency.

This prevents one cut from shutting down an entire faction.

### Complexity Rule

Tube routing is a **network planning** problem, not a pipe-placement puzzle.

The number of strategically meaningful connections must remain small enough to read at a glance.

---

## 9. Mobility

Martians possess three complementary mobility languages.

### Hover Mobility

Small Martian craft traverse rough ground efficiently.

### Walker Mobility

Walkers negotiate terrain differently from conventional wheels.

### Aero Mobility

Prepared strategic transfer between settlements.

This makes Martians extremely mobile **where they have built civilization** without making them faster everywhere than Aliens.

---

## 10. Scouting

Martians have excellent awareness close to their network.

Their reconnaissance identity combines:

- hover scouts;
- walkers;
- unusual sensor machinery;
- station-based local information.

Deep enemy reconnaissance requires deliberate mobile scouting.

The network does not automatically reveal the whole map.

---

# 11. BATTLEFIELD MANIPULATION

## Utility Mechanisms — SECONDARY STRATEGIC MECHANIC

**GAME ADAPTATION** from Martian excavation claws, articulated machines, Worker Robots, transport systems, and mechanical handling equipment.

Selected Martian assets can use physical machinery to alter local battlefield relationships.

Permitted effect families include:

### Tow

Move a friendly eligible machine out of danger or into formation.

### Redirect

Create a temporary movement preference, acceleration route, or local mechanical path effect.

### Displace

Push, pull, or drag an eligible light enemy a short distance.

### Clamp / Interfere

Temporarily impair movement or orientation without removing unit control for long periods.

### Deployment

Transform a mobile utility machine into a temporary positional apparatus.

## Hard Rule

Martian control is not a stun-lock faction.

Enemy units remain controllable.

Displacement is:

- short;
- readable;
- constrained by target size;
- usually positional rather than directly lethal.

Large heavy machines resist or ignore many displacement effects.

---

## 12. Utility

Martians possess the game's richest toolbox of non-damage mechanical interactions.

Utility may solve:

- retreat;
- reinforcement;
- rescue;
- route management;
- formation control;
- transport;
- resource routing.

This makes their complexity strategic rather than dependent on high actions per minute.

---

## 13. Combat

### Army Philosophy

Martians prefer to make an ordinary fight occur in an extraordinary position.

### Range

Mostly short-medium and medium, supported by specialized longer-range machines where competitive completeness requires them.

### Durability

Mixed.

Large walkers may be substantial.

Hover and utility machines rely on positioning.

### Damage

Moderate baseline damage.

Martian efficiency rises when:

- targets are displaced;
- routes are controlled;
- reinforcement arrives through the network;
- enemy formations are split.

### Siege

Martian siege favors:

- positional machinery;
- mechanical manipulation;
- isolating infrastructure;
- forcing defenders to move.

### Defense

Martians excel at **elastic defense**.

A threatened outpost can:

- receive network reinforcements;
- move workers away;
- reposition defenders;
- create awkward attacking geometry.

### Offense

Martian offense becomes strongest when a forward network allows:

- rapid reinforcement;
- utility support;
- retreat options.

### Defining Battlefield Behavior — MECHANICAL REPOSITIONING

The Martian army continually changes where units are relative to one another.

It is not primarily changing the terrain.

It is not changing its own equipment loadout.

It is not creating a short-lived damage surge.

It is changing **position**.

---

## 14. Technology

Martian advancement means increasing **network complexity**.

Early:

- independent worker machines;
- basic hover/walker assets;
- local settlement operation.

Mid:

- meaningful Aero Tube transfers;
- utility apparatus;
- stronger distributed production.

Late:

- highly interconnected settlements;
- advanced walkers;
- sophisticated routing;
- large mechanical control systems.

Advanced Martians should look more mechanically elaborate, not generically more futuristic.

---

## 15. Structural Strengths

- best prepared strategic reinforcement mobility;
- efficient distributed economy;
- strong positional control;
- excellent recovery through rerouting;
- unusual hover/walker movement;
- strong defense across multiple connected locations;
- ability to rescue or reposition valuable machines.

---

## 16. Structural Weaknesses

- full potential requires network setup;
- exposed links and Stations create disruption targets;
- brute-force frontal engagements are inefficient;
- deep operations far beyond the network lose many advantages;
- advanced utility demands more strategic planning than Astronaut play;
- heavy units cannot all use Aero Tube transit.

## 17. Counterplay

| Martian Advantage | Opponent Response |
|---|---|
| Aero Tube reinforcement | Scout Stations, attack links, camp predictable endpoints, or create simultaneous threats. |
| Distributed economy | Cut the network so local settlements must operate independently. |
| Displacement | Use heavy units, spread formations, attack utility machines first, or fight in open space where small displacement matters less. |
| Elastic defense | Attack multiple network branches or use siege to force Martians into predictable reinforcement exits. |
| Fast worker evacuation | Attack infrastructure or resource sites rather than chasing transported workers. |
| Network recovery | Destroy redundancy and isolate one cluster before committing to base destruction. |

# PART VIII — MATCHUP FRAMEWORK

# 1. ROCK RAIDERS VS ASTRONAUTS

### Central Strategic Tension

**Developed territory vs adaptive mobility.**

### Rock Raider Goal

Create industrial zones that the Astronauts cannot economically dislodge, then advance the serviced frontier.

### Astronaut Goal

Exploit the period before Raider infrastructure matures, attack multiple fronts, and refit around the Raider composition.

### Natural Interaction

- Raider hauling routes;
- Astronaut forward hubs;
- Raider excavation;
- air mobility;
- competition over expansions.

### Counterplay

Astronauts can force Raiders out of prepared territory.

Raiders can punish Astronaut forces that repeatedly attack mature worksites.

### Unhealthy Risk

Astronauts become capable of always selecting the perfect counter.

### Safeguard

Refits require:

- service access;
- time;
- resources.

Raider specialization therefore remains strategically valuable.

### Secondary Risk

Raiders become unbeatable once entrenched.

### Safeguard

Map resources eventually force further expansion, and heavy fortification cannot protect every economic location simultaneously.

---

# 2. ROCK RAIDERS VS ALIENS

### Central Strategic Tension

**Industrial stabilization vs offensive tempo.**

### Rock Raider Goal

Survive early/medium Alien pressure, secure Crystals, and convert the game into sustained attrition around developed worksites.

### Alien Goal

Attack before industrial momentum becomes overwhelming.

### Natural Interaction

- exposed Raider hauling;
- Alien crystal pressure;
- Raider Energy infrastructure;
- Resonance Cores;
- forward worksite establishment.

### Counterplay

Raiders use durable defenses and repair to exhaust Charge.

Aliens avoid attacking the strongest position and instead strike logistics or undeveloped expansions.

### Unhealthy Risk

Alien early pressure automatically kills Raider expansions.

### Safeguard

Basic Raider Worksites become defensible before reaching full economic efficiency.

### Opposite Risk

Aliens cannot hurt a mature Raider.

### Safeguard

Alien transformation and siege provide structure pressure, but require exposed deployment.

---

# 3. ROCK RAIDERS VS MARTIANS

### Central Strategic Tension

**Physical development vs network geometry.**

### Rock Raider Goal

Control terrain features, create stable industrial fronts, and methodically remove important Martian network positions.

### Martian Goal

Avoid attacking Raider strength directly; use network mobility and utility to threaten disconnected parts of the industrial operation.

### Natural Interaction

- excavated routes;
- Martian Tube Links;
- Raider forward service;
- network Stations;
- heavy machinery.

### Counterplay

Raiders can attack network nodes with strong siege.

Martians can rapidly reinforce whichever part of the industrial perimeter is weakest.

### Unhealthy Risk

Too many map/path mechanics create visual chaos.

### Safeguard

Rock Raider excavation uses pre-authored terrain features.

Martian Tube Links exist only between explicit Stations.

Neither system permits arbitrary terrain drawing.

---

# 4. ASTRONAUTS VS ALIENS

### Central Strategic Tension

**Prepared answer vs initiative.**

### Astronaut Goal

Scout Alien commitment early enough to create the correct combined-arms defense, survive Charge windows, then exploit Alien downtime.

### Alien Goal

Change threat direction faster than the Astronaut can refit and reposition.

### Natural Interaction

- scouting;
- Service Hubs;
- Resonance Cores;
- air control;
- transformation states.

### Counterplay

Astronaut information reduces surprise.

Alien mobility makes information decay quickly.

### Unhealthy Risk

Excellent Astronaut scouting plus refit removes all Alien strategic uncertainty.

### Safeguard

Seeing an Alien strategy identifies the problem but does not grant a free instant answer.

Refitting has material and temporal cost.

---

# 5. ASTRONAUTS VS MARTIANS

### Central Strategic Tension

**General mobility vs prepared mobility.**

### Astronaut Goal

Use air, transports, and flexible composition to attack places the Martian network cannot efficiently cover.

### Martian Goal

Establish enough network geometry that every important Astronaut attack can be met by rapid local reinforcement and positional manipulation.

### Natural Interaction

- Tube Stations;
- air routes;
- forward Service Hubs;
- Martian utility machines;
- remote expansions.

### Counterplay

Astronauts threaten links and Stations.

Martians punish Astronaut formations that enter prepared control zones.

### Unhealthy Risk

Astronaut aircraft simply bypass all Martian mechanics.

### Safeguard

Martians possess complete anti-air functionality and distributed defensive capability.

Aircraft still require surface objectives and service infrastructure.

---

# 6. ALIENS VS MARTIANS

### Central Strategic Tension

**Tempo vs network depth.**

### Alien Goal

Hit key Martian nodes before redundancy forms, then force the Martian army to react faster than Tube routing can stabilize the front.

### Martian Goal

Survive initial attacks, preserve network redundancy, and convert Alien aggression into unfavorable positional fights.

### Natural Interaction

- Resonance Cores;
- Tube Stations;
- high-speed harassment;
- endpoint defense;
- transformation;
- displacement.

### Counterplay

Aliens can bypass ordinary approaches.

Martians can rapidly redeploy inside prepared territory.

### Unhealthy Risk

Martian displacement creates frustrating control denial against fragile Alien units.

### Safeguard

Hard displacement rules:

- short duration;
- size restrictions;
- telegraphing;
- no repeated stun lock.

### Opposite Risk

Alien mobility invalidates the Tube Network.

### Safeguard

The network moves economy and reinforcements faster than ordinary field movement and therefore retains macro value even against highly mobile opponents.

# PART IX — SHARED RTS LANGUAGE

Strong asymmetry does not extend to every interface convention.

## Standardized Across All Four Factions

### Selection

- left-click selection;
- box selection;
- control groups;
- double-click/type selection;
- standard deselection behavior.

### Basic Orders

Every relevant unit uses recognizable:

- Move;
- Attack;
- Attack-Move;
- Stop;
- Hold;
- Patrol;
- Rally;
- Repair/interact where applicable.

Faction mechanics may add orders.

They do not redefine what “Move” means.

### Resource Language

Ore/Metal, Energy, and Crystals use globally recognizable icons and strategic meanings.

Faction-specific derived mechanics such as Alien Charge receive separate UI treatment.

### Health

Health, damage state, repairability, shields if later used, and disabled states must be visually understandable regardless of faction.

### Production

Players can always identify:

- what is building;
- remaining progress;
- why production is blocked;
- where the result will appear.

### Technology

Locked requirements and current research remain explicit.

### Attack Feedback

The player can identify:

- intended target;
- attack range where relevant;
- inability to attack a target class;
- major status effects.

### Expansion Readability

A player must be able to identify an enemy expansion even if its construction method is unfamiliar.

### Fog of War

All factions obey the same fundamental visibility/fog framework.

Faction tools may interact differently with it.

### Transport

Entering, exiting, or transferring through transport systems uses consistent destination and capacity feedback.

### Status Language

Major states such as:

- disconnected;
- unserviced;
- refitting;
- overcharged;
- transforming;
- in Tube transit;

receive strong icons and world-space feedback.

# PART XIV — CANON INTEGRATION & SELF-AUDIT

## Contradiction Audit

No Phase 02 rule replaces a locked Phase 01 principle.

Instead:

- Rock Raider terrain interaction remains subject to universal competitive topology;
- Astronaut technological lineages remain parallel rather than sequential;
- Alien crystals amplify advanced play without becoming necessary for basic faction operation;
- Martian identity remains connectivity-based rather than crystal-based;
- all factions continue to care about economy, expansion, production, scouting, composition, positioning, and map control.

## Generic Science-Fiction Audit

The principal potentially invented systems are:

- Rock Raider Worksite servicing;
- Astronaut shared Service infrastructure;
- Alien Resonance Cores and deployable infrastructure;
- expanded Martian Station/network architecture.

Each exists because a complete RTS requires infrastructure beyond literal source-set reproduction.

Each has a defined source-material motif.

Generic tanks, barracks, alien hives, contemporary weapon platforms, and generic military bases remain prohibited.

## Faction-Overlap Audit

The four macro verbs remain distinct:

**Raiders develop locations.**

**Astronauts adapt assets.**

**Aliens intensify timing.**

**Martians connect locations.**

No two primary mechanics solve the same strategic problem.

## Counterplay Audit

Every primary mechanic has visible interaction:

- Worksites can be isolated or attacked;
- refitting creates cost and downtime;
- Resonance Cores expose Crystal commitments;
- Aero Tube networks expose Stations and Links.

No primary mechanic exists solely inside invisible UI.

## Complexity Audit

Exactly one primary unique macro mechanic is assigned to each faction.

Secondary mechanics reinforce the primary identity rather than creating unrelated minigames.

Basic RTS orders remain shared.

## Source Compatibility Audit

The architecture accommodates all major source categories while intentionally leaving exact asset mapping to Phase 03.

The Phase 00 distinction between released entry, underlying design, and future RTS asset remains intact.

# CANON ESTABLISHED BY PHASE 02

The following decisions are now authoritative for all later phases.

1. **Rock Raiders use the Worksite Network as their primary faction macro system.**

2. Worksites connect industrial service, hauling, processing, production, construction, and repair without requiring manual resource-hauler micro.

3. Rock Raider expansions are comparatively investment-heavy but become exceptionally productive and defensible when mature.

4. Rock Raiders use authored **Excavatable Terrain Features** rather than arbitrary freeform competitive-map excavation.

5. Excavated strategic routes become universally usable once opened; no victory-critical map content is Raider-exclusive.

6. The defining Rock Raider battlefield behavior is the **Industrial Advance**.

7. Rock Raider combat remains derived primarily from mining, engineering, drilling, hauling, excavation, demolition, and industrial machinery.

8. **Astronauts contain two equal parallel engineering traditions:** Life on Mars-derived **Field Systems** and Mars Mission-derived **Mission Systems**.

9. Life on Mars technology is not an early tier and Mars Mission technology is not a late tier.

10. Field Systems emphasize rugged exploration, sustainment, surface operation, and field modularity.

11. Mission Systems emphasize high-performance aerospace, specialized extraction, transformation, command, and mission-specific capability.

12. Both Astronaut engineering traditions must remain strategically visible during normal competitive play.

13. **Mission Refit** is the Astronaut primary macro mechanic.

14. Only a curated subset of assets is configurable; modularity does not become universal ability spam.

15. Refitting requires appropriate service infrastructure, time, and resource expenditure.

16. **Forward Service Network** is the Astronaut secondary strategic mechanic.

17. Astronauts possess the broadest conventional scouting and air capability, but aircraft cannot replace ground territorial control.

18. The defining Astronaut battlefield behavior is **Combined-Arms Response**.

19. **Crystal Charge** is the Alien primary macro mechanic.

20. Alien Crystals have two competing uses: permanent advanced expenditure and reversible commitment to Charge generation.

21. **Charge is a derived faction resource, not a fourth strategic map resource.**

22. Charge powers selected high-intensity production, mobility, transformation, and combat systems rather than every basic Alien action.

23. Alien Resonance Cores are **NEW GAME CONTENT** explicitly authorized as source-consistent extrapolation from Mars Mission Alien crystal, ETX, containment, and Mothership motifs.

24. Attacking Resonance Cores provides direct counterplay against Alien Crystal commitment.

25. Alien buildings are primarily spacecraft-derived deployable or unfolded technological structures rather than generic alien buildings.

26. **Deploy / Reconfigure** is the Alien secondary strategic mechanic.

27. The defining Alien battlefield behavior is the **Surge Window**.

28. Alien aggression emerges from resource commitment, mobility, and transformation rather than artificially cheap swarm units.

29. Aliens are a technological fleet civilization with biomechanical craft, not a biological swarm gameplay faction (Phase 02A).

30. **The Aero Tube Network is the Martian primary macro mechanic.**

31. Martian expansion represents extension of an established settlement civilization rather than establishment of a temporary expedition.

32. Aero Tube Stations route resources across connected settlements.

33. Aero Tubes also transfer a limited category of crew and smaller eligible machines.

34. Large combat machines do not universally use Tube transfer.

35. Tube movement has travel time, visible endpoints, visible network structure, and opponent counterplay.

36. Disconnected Martian settlements continue functioning locally rather than shutting down completely.

37. Martian Tube construction is node-to-node network planning, not arbitrary pipe drawing.

38. **Utility Mechanisms** are the Martian secondary strategic mechanic.

39. Martian mechanical control may tow allies, redirect movement, deploy machinery, and modestly displace eligible enemies.

40. Martian battlefield control cannot rely on prolonged stun-lock or removal of player control.

41. The defining Martian battlefield behavior is **Mechanical Repositioning**.

42. Alien and Martian differentiation is permanently defined as:

**Aliens manipulate power and tempo.**

**Martians manipulate connectivity and position.**

43. Rock Raider and Astronaut differentiation is permanently defined as:

**Rock Raiders transform and industrialize the environment.**

**Astronauts transform and reconfigure mission capability.**

44. Standard competitive UI conventions remain shared across factions even when underlying economic and construction rules differ.

45. Each faction has an explicit complexity budget consisting of one primary macro mechanic, one secondary strategic mechanic, and one defining battlefield behavior.

46. No Phase 03 roster may introduce additional faction-wide systems casually; any such proposal must demonstrate that an existing Phase 02 system cannot already solve the problem.

47. Source-set physical size does not determine unit tier or competitive importance.

48. Official promotional, duplicate, bundle, accessory, mixed, and character material remains eligible for meaningful non-unit coverage.

49. No faction is required to solve each RTS function with a separate dedicated unit.

50. All six matchups must preserve reciprocal counterplay and cannot be decided automatically by faction selection.
