# PART I — SETTING IDENTITY

# 1. FRONTIER NAME

The broader playable region is canonically named:

# THE MERIDIAN REACH

In ordinary dialogue and UI it may be shortened to:

**the Reach.**

The name applies to the connected frontier region rather than to one planet, faction, or war.

It is suitable for:

- campaign maps;
- mission briefings;
- navigation displays;
- strategic reports;
- multiplayer environment naming;
- marketing.

Examples:

- *Operations in the Meridian Reach*
- *Meridian Reach Campaign*
- *Crystal activity along the outer Reach*
- *Rock Raider survey teams have crossed into the Reach*
- *Martian settlements along the Meridian routes*

The name is **NEW GAME CONTINUITY**, not official LEGO terminology.

---

# 2. SETTING SCALE

The Meridian Reach is a **compact four-system interstellar corridor**.

It consists of:

- the Solar System;
- three nearby frontier star systems;
- the stable navigation routes, asteroid belts, stations, moons, and expedition zones connecting them.

This is the smallest useful setting scale that simultaneously preserves:

- Mars as an important historical location;
- distinct Life on Mars and Mars Mission foundations;
- deep-space Rock Raider operations;
- Alien-controlled territory away from Mars;
- multiple planets and moons;
- asteroid operations;
- meaningful travel distance;
- geographically understandable campaign progression.

The game does **not** take place across an entire galaxy.

Travel across the Reach is significant but routine enough for:

- expeditions;
- mining operations;
- military deployments;
- trade;
- migration;
- fleet movement.

Individual worlds therefore retain identity.

---

# 3. FRONTIER GEOGRAPHY

The Reach is organized into six broad strategic regions.

## THE INNER ROUTES

The most established human navigation corridors surrounding the Solar System.

Characteristics:

- mature navigation;
- research and logistics traffic;
- fewer completely unknown worlds;
- extensive Astronaut operations;
- regular Rock Raider contracts.

Mars sits at the frontier-facing edge of this region rather than at its safe center.

---

## THE MARTIAN ARC

Mars and the network of later Martian settlements established beyond it.

This is not an exclusive Martian ethnic territory.

It is the region where Martian:

- settlements;
- civic machinery;
- Tube infrastructure;
- historical influence

are most common.

Human and Raider installations also exist here.

---

## THE INDUSTRIAL FRONTIER

Rocky worlds, asteroid zones, deep caverns, and resource belts connected primarily through mining and survey routes.

Common activity:

- Rock Raider extraction;
- Astronaut survey bases;
- independent industrial stations;
- disputed mineral claims.

---

## THE CRYSTAL VERGE

A discontinuous chain of crystal-rich worlds, moons, impact zones, and asteroid formations.

It is strategically valuable to every faction.

Mars Mission Alien operations are particularly concentrated here.

The Crystal Verge is not Alien biological territory.

It is a resource geography around which Alien technological presence became especially strong.

---

## THE TRANSIT MARCHES

Thinly settled routes lying between major inhabited regions.

They contain:

- abandoned expeditions;
- relay stations;
- isolated settlements;
- old mining sites;
- small asteroid facilities;
- unfinished infrastructure.

These regions provide much of the game's neutral frontier geography.

---

## THE OUTER FRACTURE

The least stable charted portion of the Reach.

Typical environments include:

- volcanically active worlds;
- fragmented moons;
- unstable asteroid regions;
- frozen outer-system bodies;
- crystal-disrupted terrain.

Alien fleets, Raiders, Astronaut expeditions, and Martian outposts can all appear here.

# 4. CHRONOLOGY FRAMEWORK

## GAME CONTINUITY / ADAPTATION

The following chronology exists only for LEGO Space RTS.

It does **not** claim that LEGO officially established a unified chronology between Rock Raiders, Life on Mars, and Mars Mission.

---

## THE DEEP SURVEY ERA

Rock Raider operations become established as specialized deep-space mining and engineering expeditions.

Their crews learn to operate in:

- unstable caverns;
- hostile geology;
- distant mining zones;
- dangerous resource environments.

They remain a specialist industrial organization rather than a sovereign state.

This era establishes the Rock Raider tradition represented by the original source theme.

---

## THE FIRST MARS CONTACT ERA

Human surface expeditions establish sustained operations on Mars and encounter the existing Martian civilization.

The rugged exploratory engineering associated with Life on Mars becomes foundational to long-duration off-world field operations.

Human and Martian contact includes periods of:

- curiosity;
- technical exchange;
- misunderstanding;
- cooperation;
- territorial tension.

This era establishes the Life on Mars human and Martian source foundations.

---

## THE MARS MISSION ERA

Large crystal discoveries and increasingly dangerous frontier encounters cause human exploration organizations to develop more specialized high-output mission equipment.

The white/orange Mars Mission technological tradition expands around:

- aerospace operations;
- crystal extraction;
- modular mission vehicles;
- mobile command systems;
- high-performance drilling.

During this period, major encounters with the technological Mars Mission Aliens bring crystal competition into open conflict.

This is an adaptation of the Mars Mission source foundation rather than a claim about official chronology.

---

## THE MERIDIAN CONVERGENCE — PRESENT DAY

Navigation, scanning, and long-range logistics now make all four systems of the Reach strategically interconnected.

Previously separated operating zones overlap.

Rock Raiders follow new geological surveys.

Astronauts establish expeditions throughout the frontier.

Martian settlements expand beyond Mars.

Alien crystal operations extend along the Crystal Verge.

The playable RTS occurs during this period.

---

# 5. ASTRONAUT INSTITUTIONAL HISTORY

The modern Astronaut faction is organized through the:

# ASTRONAUT EXPEDITIONARY SERVICE

This is **NEW GAME CONTINUITY**.

It is the institutional framework connecting both human engineering traditions.

The Service inherited two equally legitimate technological schools.

## FIELD SYSTEMS

Derived from the Life on Mars human tradition.

They developed around:

- remote exploration;
- unreliable supply conditions;
- rugged terrain;
- solar operation;
- modular physical payloads;
- long-duration surface expeditions.

Field Systems remain strategically valuable because frontier environments often reward:

- durability;
- simple servicing;
- remote operation;
- efficient surface mobility;
- low logistical burden.

---

## MISSION SYSTEMS

Derived from the later Mars Mission engineering tradition.

They developed around:

- high-output aerospace;
- specialized extraction;
- rapid deployment;
- transformation;
- advanced command systems;
- dangerous crystal operations.

Mission Systems remain strategically valuable because frontier conflicts often require:

- high performance;
- specialized combat;
- airlift;
- heavy drilling;
- rapid mission reconfiguration.

---

## MODERN INTEGRATION

Neither tradition replaced the other.

Instead, shared:

- command protocols;
- docking standards;
- module interfaces;
- logistics systems;
- crew training

allow both to operate within one expedition.

Field Systems answer:

**How does the expedition remain useful far from ideal infrastructure?**

Mission Systems answer:

**How does the expedition perform a specialized high-intensity task?**

This institutional history directly supports the Phase 02–04 rule that both lineages remain relevant throughout a normal match.

# 6. MARTIAN CIVILIZATION

Martians are an established technological civilization whose built environment developed around **distributed settlements** rather than enormous centralized cities.

Settlement patterns commonly include:

- clustered habitation/work modules;
- excavation sites;
- mechanical workshops;
- local civic machinery;
- resource handling stations;
- transport junctions;
- smaller settlements connected to larger hubs.

Their technology is visibly specialized because Martian engineering culture favors:

**a mechanism designed perfectly for a particular relationship or task**

over:

**one standardized chassis forced to solve everything.**

This produces:

- unusual walkers;
- hover machinery;
- articulated tools;
- narrow specialist vehicles;
- elaborate transport mechanisms.

## WHY AERO TUBES EXIST

Aero Tube technology originally developed as a practical solution for moving:

- personnel;
- small machines;
- resources;
- maintenance equipment

between dispersed settlements across terrain where conventional roads are:

- inefficient;
- vulnerable;
- difficult to maintain.

Tube infrastructure is therefore simultaneously:

- civic;
- industrial;
- logistical.

Its battlefield use is an adaptation of normal Martian settlement technology.

Martians are not automatically hostile to humans.

Relations vary by:

- settlement;
- historical claim;
- resource dispute;
- political circumstance;
- immediate security concern.

No deeper language, religion, or universal political structure is established in Phase 05.

---

# 7. MARS MISSION ALIEN CIVILIZATION

Mars Mission Aliens are a technological civilization whose frontier presence is strongly associated with:

- crystal acquisition;
- mobile craft;
- deployable technological footholds;
- fleet-linked infrastructure;
- transformation.

Their exact homeworld and total civilization scale remain deliberately undefined.

What is canonically established is that they possess:

- organized technological industry;
- command structures;
- fleets;
- long-term crystal operations;
- territorial priorities;
- the capacity to establish persistent bases.

Alien frontier infrastructure frequently feels fleet-derived because their civilization is especially comfortable treating:

- spacecraft;
- production systems;
- planetary installations

as variations of the same technology.

Crystals are a valuable technological resource.

They are **not** objects of mystical worship.

Aliens are **not**:

- a hive species;
- biological swarm organisms;
- inherently evil invaders.

Some Alien operations are aggressively expansionist.

Others may be:

- defensive;
- territorial;
- extractive;
- retaliatory.

The player must be able to understand conflict from an Alien perspective.

---

# 8. ROCK RAIDER OPERATING CONTEXT

Rock Raiders are a trans-system industrial exploration organization.

They undertake:

- mining contracts;
- deep-resource surveys;
- rescue operations;
- geological engineering;
- hazardous excavation;
- infrastructure recovery.

They maintain mobile expedition capability across the Reach because their expertise is valuable on worlds that ordinary industrial equipment cannot exploit safely.

They are not a sovereign military empire.

Rock Raiders become involved in armed conflict when:

- mineral rights are disputed;
- crews are attacked;
- critical mining infrastructure must be defended;
- dangerous regions overlap another faction's expansion;
- resource competition escalates;
- a client operation becomes part of a larger conflict.

Their combat capability remains an extension of industrial survival and engineering.

# 1. SUBTERRANEAN MINING CAVERNS

## GEOLOGY

## TERRAIN STRUCTURE

- chambers;
- broad tunnels;
- natural ramps;
- rock bridges;
- branching passages.

## RESOURCE CHARACTER

Excellent Ore density.

Crystals occur in rarer deep formations.

## COMMON HAZARDS

- rockfalls;
- steam/pressure vents;
- unstable crystal formations.

## EXCAVATABLE FEATURES

Very common.

## BUILDING SPACE

Large chambers provide useful but bounded construction areas.

## MOVEMENT IMPLICATIONS

Walkers and hover benefit from rubble-heavy surfaces.

## AIR IMPLICATIONS

## FACTION INTERACTION

**Rock Raiders:** strongest thematic fit and many excavation opportunities.

**Astronauts:** survey and modular field operations remain effective.

**Aliens:** compact footholds exploit chamber intersections.

**Martians:** networks can connect cavern settlements but Tube routes must respect rock volumes.

## CAMPAIGN POTENTIAL

Exceptional.

## COMPETITIVE SUITABILITY

Standard and specialized multiplayer, provided passages remain broad and universal topology is preserved.

# PART III — UNIVERSAL TERRAIN TAXONOMY

Implementation uses a small number of **base pathing surfaces** plus orthogonal terrain flags.

This prevents every biome from inventing unique movement rules.

---

# BASE PATHING SURFACES

| Category | Visual readability | Movement | Building | Air | Can change? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal Ground** | Clean stable surface | All ground classes | Usually buildable where flagged | No effect | Normally no |
| **Rough Ground** | Broken rock, rubble, ice, uneven terrain | Wheels slower; tracks/walkers/hover largely unaffected | Usually non-buildable until specifically cleared | No effect | Some authored patches can be cleared |
| **Shallow Liquid** | Clearly visible shallow depth and shoreline | Hover crosses; amphibious crosses; conventional ground blocked | Non-buildable | Air ignores | Normally no |
| **Deep Liquid** | Darker/deeper visual treatment | Only amphibious/surface-skimmer among ground classes | Non-buildable | Air ignores | No |
| **Impassable Formation** | Major rock, chasm, void, solid wall | Blocks all ground | Non-buildable | Does not automatically block air | Usually no |

---

# CONNECTORS

## RAMP

Connects elevation bands.

- pathable to all normal ground classes;
- normally buildable only at broad flat ends;
- visually wider and smoother than surrounding cliff;
- does not affect true air.

## BRIDGE / PLATFORM

Artificial or natural pathable surface above another terrain type.

- belongs to a normal ground pathing region;
- can connect equivalent or different elevations;
- may be non-buildable;
- competitive critical bridges are not casually destructible.

---

# SPATIAL TAGS

## NARROW PASSAGE

A designer topology tag rather than its own movement surface.

Used for:

- AI;
- tactical map analysis;
- choke classification.

## OPEN FIELD

A topology tag indicating enough contiguous ground for full medium-scale army maneuver.

---

# BUILDABILITY FLAGS

## BUILDABLE

Terrain accepts normal structures.

## NON-BUILDABLE

Movement may remain possible but structures cannot be placed.

Common uses:

- roads;
- resource aprons;
- ramps;
- narrow corridors;
- decorative strategic lanes.

Buildability is visible through surface shape and world design rather than a permanent glowing grid.

---

# DYNAMIC TERRAIN FLAGS

## EXCAVATABLE FEATURE

Rock Raider-authored terrain interaction.

Starts blocked.

Can be permanently opened during the match.

## DESTRUCTIBLE OBJECT

Universal combat interaction.

May remove an obstacle or environmental object.

## HAZARD ZONE

Predictable environmental gameplay effect.

## AIR-BLOCKING VOLUME

Blocks true-air pathing.

Used for:

- low cavern ceilings;
- large enclosed tunnels;
- orbital walls;
- major overhangs;
- map boundaries.

# PART IV — ELEVATION & LINE OF SIGHT

The game uses a **discrete elevation-band model**.

Standard competitive maps normally contain:

- Low Ground;
- High Ground;
- raised platforms/bridges.

Caverns may visually contain much larger vertical spaces, but combat navigation still resolves through discrete playable elevation surfaces.

---

# LOW GROUND

Standard baseline terrain.

---

# HIGH GROUND

One elevation band above adjacent low ground.

High ground provides:

- better visibility over surrounding low terrain;
- cleaner firing sightlines;
- defensive spatial value.

It does **not** directly provide:

- bonus weapon damage;
- bonus accuracy;
- automatic attack-range increase;
- arbitrary evasion.

This avoids copying another RTS's high-ground combat formula.

---

# CLIFFS

Cliffs:

- block ground movement;
- block building;
- act as line-of-sight occluders for ground observers;
- do not inherently block surface true-air movement.

A unit standing on low ground cannot see through a cliff.

---

# RAMPS

Ramps are the normal ground connection between elevation bands.

A major competitive plateau must normally have:

- at least one broad ground ramp;
- preferably two attack relationships unless it is intentionally a modest defensive position.

---

# BRIDGES AND PLATFORMS

Bridges do not create an additional combat layer.

They are ordinary navigable surfaces with an assigned elevation band.

---

# VISION RULE

High-ground units receive a modest terrain-derived vision advantage across adjacent low ground.

The prototype authoring assumption is approximately:

**10–15% greater effective visual horizon where the high-ground edge is unobstructed.**

No unit's weapon range automatically changes because of elevation.

Actual unit vision values remain a later balance parameter.

---

# WHY THIS MODEL FITS

The system makes:

- cliffs;
- plateaus;
- ramps;
- overlooks

strategically meaningful without requiring hidden probability or ballistic simulation.

The player can understand elevation directly from the world.

One build-grid cell is treated as approximately **2 world meters** for prototype layout planning.

This is an authoring relationship rather than a demand that every LEGO object obey literal real-world scale.

# 4v4 POLICY

4v4 is supported for:

- custom games;
- social play;
- selected large maps.

It is **not a core ranked-map standard**.

Eight 100-OC factions simultaneously create excessive:

- battlefield density;
- pathing pressure;
- visual noise;
- infrastructure overlap

for the intended medium-scale RTS.

Core competitive map development therefore prioritizes:

1. 1v1;
2. 2v2;
3. 3v3.

# PART VI — STARTING BASE GEOMETRY

Every normal competitive starting location must support the most spatially demanding faction rather than being sized for the most compact one.

## MINIMUM STARTING FOOTPRINT

A main base receives approximately:

**42 × 42 cells of practical contiguous base space**

before accounting for purely decorative edge terrain.

At least:

**34 × 34 cells**

must remain broadly usable for structures and circulation.

---

# STARTING COMMAND POSITION

The command structure sits:

- approximately 8–12 cells from the starting Ore access face;
- away from a cliff edge;
- with at least two usable structure-placement directions.

---

# STARTING ORE

Starting Ore is:

- close enough for a functional opening;
- not directly touching the command structure;
- positioned so workers cannot be trivially trapped.

A clear extraction apron of approximately **6 cells** remains around the active access face.

---

# GENERATOR SPACE

Every main has room for at least:

- two normal Energy structures;
- one additional late generator

without consuming the only rally or production lane.

---

# PRODUCTION SPACE

The starting footprint must support simultaneously:

- two normal production structures;
- one advanced/large production structure;
- expansion of faction-specific service infrastructure.

---

# RALLY SPACE

At least one **12-cell-wide** internal circulation/rally lane connects the base interior to its principal exit.

This supports:

- Chrome Crusher;
- large Astronaut vehicles;
- Red Planet machinery;
- normal medium army movement.

---

# AIRCRAFT CLEARANCE

Standard surface main bases provide:

- at least one 14 × 14-cell clear aircraft operations apron;
- no permanent air-blocking volume immediately above the command area.

---

# DEFENSIVE EDGES

A starting base may have cliffs or impassable terrain on portions of its perimeter.

It must not be enclosed so completely that one faction's compact defense is disproportionately favored.

A standard main normally has:

- one principal broad approach;
- at least one secondary pressure route.

---

# PART VII — ORE GEOGRAPHY

Phase 04's Ore categories remain authoritative:

- Small — approximately 600 Ore;
- Standard — approximately 900;
- Rich — approximately 1,350;
- Deep contested seam — approximately 2,400.

---

# STARTING ORE

Target:

**approximately 1,800 safe Ore per player.**

Typical arrangements:

- two Standard deposits;
- one Rich + one Small;
- three Small deposits.

The deposits should not all share the exact same worker approach.

---

# FIRST-EXPANSION ORE

Typical value:

**1,350–1,800 Ore.**

The first expansion becomes attractive before the main is depleted.

It should normally be:

- closer to its owner than the enemy;
- attackable;
- less defensible than the main.

---

# SECONDARY ORE

Typical site value:

**900–1,350 Ore.**

Secondary expansions are:

- more exposed;
- farther from main reinforcement;
- often adjacent to an important route.

---

# DEEP ORE

Deep contested seams are late-game anchors.

Standard 1v1 maps normally contain:

**one or two 2,400-Ore Deep Seams.**

They are deliberately difficult to hold.

A Deep Seam may appear:

- centrally;
- on opposite side sectors;
- along a lateral route;
- in exposed high ground;
- behind a wide contested basin.

It is **not required** to sit in the geometric center.

---

# STANDARD 1V1 ORE-SITE COUNT

Typical map:

- 2 mains;
- 2 naturals;
- 2–4 secondary expansions;
- 1–2 Deep Ore locations.

Total:

**7–10 meaningful Ore sites.**

---

# ORE & CHOKEPOINTS

Ore access must not sit directly inside a tiny choke.

Workers need operating room.

Defending an expansion may involve controlling a choke, but the deposit itself should normally sit behind or beside the contested approach rather than physically obstructing it.

---

# ORE & CRYSTALS

The most valuable Ore and Crystal field should not always occupy the same location.

Maps rotate among:

- Ore-rich / Crystal-poor expansions;
- Crystal-rich / modest-Ore expansions;
- high-Ore exposed sectors;
- lateral Crystal objectives.

This prevents one universal map-control answer.

---

# PART VIII — CRYSTAL GEOGRAPHY

Crystals remain:

- finite;
- scarce;
- advanced;
- contested.

There is no normal starting Crystal field.

Phase 04 Crystal deposits remain:

- Small — 5 Crystals;
- Standard — 8;
- Rich — 14;
- High-grade contested field — 20.

---

# STANDARD 1V1 CRYSTAL BUDGET

Typical total:

**28–40 Crystals**

across:

**4–6 distinct pockets.**

This creates enough strategic access for advanced play without making Crystal control trivial.

---

# SMALL CRYSTAL POCKET

**5 Crystals**

Common use:

- near a lateral expansion;
- side route;
- modest risk area.

Frequency:

**1–3 per standard 1v1.**

---

# STANDARD CRYSTAL FIELD

**8 Crystals**

Common use:

- near a first-expansion region;
- exposed side sector;
- contested mid-map lane.

Frequency:

**1–3.**

---

# RICH CRYSTAL FIELD

**14 Crystals**

Common use:

- forward expansion;
- central-side objective;
- exposed high ground.

Frequency:

**0–2.**

---

# HIGH-GRADE CONTESTED FIELD

**20 Crystals**

Rare.

Normally:

**0 or 1 per 1v1 map.**

A High-Grade Field replaces several smaller Crystal opportunities rather than being added on top of an already rich Crystal map.

---

# GEOGRAPHIC RULES

Crystals should appear in varied relationships:

- adjacent to but not inside a natural;
- between two secondaries;
- on a lateral route;
- behind a broad exposed approach;
- near a predictable hazard;
- in an optional resource pocket.

At least one meaningful Crystal opportunity on a normal map should be contestable without crossing the exact geometric center.

---

# SIX-MATCHUP CRYSTAL TEST

## ROCK RAIDERS VS ASTRONAUTS

Risk:

Astronaut mobility repeatedly steals distant crystals while Raider heavy forces cannot respond.

Safeguard:

Crystal sites require nearby ground-control space and are not positioned exclusively on extreme perimeter air routes.

---

## ROCK RAIDERS VS ALIENS

Risk:

Aliens convert early Crystal access into repeated pressure before Raider Worksites mature.

Safeguard:

The first major Alien Crystal investment must require exposed territory; no rich field is safely tucked behind an Alien-side natural.

---

## ROCK RAIDERS VS MARTIANS

Risk:

Martian Tube geometry could connect every important Crystal site more efficiently than Raider infrastructure can contest them.

Safeguard:

Major fields are not positioned in a straight chain of naturally safe Station pads.

---

## ASTRONAUTS VS ALIENS

Risk:

Either Astronaut aircraft or Alien pressure gains an uncontestable lateral Crystal field.

Safeguard:

Every meaningful Crystal field has normal ground access and viable anti-air coverage positions.

---

## ASTRONAUTS VS MARTIANS

Risk:

Astronaut airlift or Martian networks make Crystal denial too binary.

Safeguard:

Fields have several approach directions and enough ground area for conventional armies.

---

## ALIENS VS MARTIANS

Risk:

A compact Alien Core or protected Martian Station sits directly on the Crystal deposit and becomes excessively safe.

Safeguard:

The deposit access apron and main expansion build pad are separated by approximately 6–10 cells so resource control and base fortification are related but not identical.

---

# PART IX — ENERGY SPACE

There are no normal competitive Energy nodes.

Energy geography is therefore **construction geography**.

Maps must provide physical room for:

- Rock Raider Power Stations;
- Astronaut Solar Energy Arrays;
- Alien Power Couplers;
- Martian Pressure Generators.

---

# GENERATOR PLACEMENT RULES

Generator placement should involve:

- exposure;
- base layout;
- production-space tradeoffs.

It should **not** involve discovering a faction-specific map node.

---

# ENVIRONMENTAL ENERGY MODIFIERS

Standard competitive biomes do not alter:

- Energy generation;
- Energy reserve;
- generator output.

No ordinary desert map makes Solar Arrays better.

No cavern map makes them worse.

No crystal biome secretly improves Alien power.

Environmental Energy modifiers are reserved for:

- campaign missions;
- explicitly labeled special multiplayer scenarios.

Moderate.

Routes:

Normally one broad main connection plus at least one pressure relationship.

Minimum 34 × 34 practical site.

Low-risk continued economy.

Distance:

Moderate.

Supports economic play without being as efficient or valuable as contested territory.

Purpose:

Economy plus staging position.

Moderate–Rich Ore.

Defensibility:

Low–moderate.

Multiple enemy-facing approaches.

Shortens reinforcement and service distance.

Trade accessibility for local positional strength.

Normally moderate rather than maximum.

Good, but never one-ramp fortress geometry on standard maps.

Deliberately exposed.

# 6. DEEP ORE EXPANSION

Purpose:

Late-game economic anchor.

Deep contested seam.

# 7. RISK / HIGH-YIELD EXPANSION

Purpose:

Map-specific greed opportunity.

At least two attack relationships.

# PART XI — ROCK RAIDER SPATIAL DESIGN

The map must make Worksite layout meaningful without being designed specifically for Raiders.

---

# WORKSITE GEOMETRY

Phase 04 service radii remain:

- Rock Raiders HQ — 18 cells;
- Vehicle Service Bay — 12 cells.

Normal expansion sites therefore contain enough usable space for a player to decide whether to:

- place HQ near resources;
- place HQ more centrally;
- extend service with a Vehicle Service Bay;
- place processing near the haul route;
- place production deeper in safety.

The map should not allow one obvious placement to service the entire resource field, production zone, generator zone, and defensive perimeter perfectly.

---

# HAULING ROUTES

Resource-facing sites require:

- broad worker/hauler circulation;
- no one-cell industrial funnels;
- no decorative collision maze between Ore and processing.

Typical mature haul path from extraction face to processing should remain roughly:

**8–22 cells.**

Very long paths are intentional inefficiencies.

---

# HEAVY MACHINE ACCESS

Any normal Raider expansion must have at least one ground route usable by:

- Loader Dozer;
- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher.

A nominally buildable site that cannot admit major Raider machinery is invalid as a standard expansion.

---

# EXPANSION MATURATION

Maps should create choices between:

- placing the HQ closer to Ore;
- protecting Power and Processing;
- preserving rally space;
- extending Worksite coverage toward a forward route.

This makes industrial planning meaningful without creating Raider-exclusive resource sites.

---

# PART XII — EXCAVATABLE TERRAIN SYSTEM

Competitive excavation is authored.

There is no voxel digging.

Five canonical Excavatable Terrain classes exist.

---

# 1. LOOSE RUBBLE BLOCKAGE

## VISUAL LANGUAGE

Clearly collapsed loose stones and broken LEGO-geological chunks.

## WHO CAN INTERACT

- Rock Raider Crew;
- Loader Dozer;
- Drill Craft;
- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher.

## TOOL REQUIREMENT

Crew can clear this class manually.

Machines clear it faster.

## TIME CLASS

Very short.

Approximately:

- Crew: 20–30 seconds;
- industrial machine: 8–15 seconds.

## ECONOMIC COST

No formal Excavation Energy cost for Crew clearance.

Machine clearing uses normal operating activity but no 25-Energy drilling charge.

## RESULT

- opens small route;
- clears local building/rally space.

## COUNTERPLAY

Opponents can contest the clearing location.

## COMPETITIVE FREQUENCY

Common.

---

# 2. FRACTURED ROCK WALL

## VISUAL LANGUAGE

A continuous rock wall showing:

- strong cracks;
- exposed drillable layers;
- recognizable Raider survey markings when selected.

## WHO CAN INTERACT

- Drill Craft;
- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher.

## TOOL REQUIREMENT

Powered drilling.

## TIME CLASS

Short–medium.

## ECONOMIC COST

Approximately **25 Energy**.

## RESULT

Usually opens:

- optional flank;
- shortcut;
- second access to a region.

## COUNTERPLAY

Drilling is noisy, visible, and stationary enough to contest.

## COMPETITIVE FREQUENCY

Common–moderate.

---

# 3. MINERALIZED RIDGE

## VISUAL LANGUAGE

Rock barrier containing visibly embedded:

- Ore;
- occasional crystals.

## WHO CAN INTERACT

- Drill Craft;
- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher.

## COST

Approximately **25 Energy**.

## TIME CLASS

Medium.

## RESULT

May expose:

- Small Ore pocket;
- small Crystal pocket;
- additional buildable shelf.

It never contains a mandatory resource.

## COUNTERPLAY

The revealed area becomes universally contestable.

## COMPETITIVE FREQUENCY

Moderate.

---

# 4. SEALED TUNNEL MOUTH

## VISUAL LANGUAGE

Collapsed or naturally sealed passage whose surrounding geology clearly implies an existing route beyond it.

## WHO CAN INTERACT

- Drill Craft;
- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher.

## COST

Approximately **25 Energy**.

## TIME CLASS

Medium–long.

## RESULT

Opens a substantial optional shortcut or flank.

## COUNTERPLAY

The future exit is visible on the map.

Opponents can:

- prepare defenses;
- attack drilling;
- use the tunnel after completion.

## COMPETITIVE FREQUENCY

Moderate.

---

# 5. REINFORCED BEDROCK BARRIER

## VISUAL LANGUAGE

Dense dark geological formation with visibly harder internal structure.

## WHO CAN INTERACT

- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher;
- Drill Craft only at very poor efficiency.

## COST

Approximately **50 Energy**.

## TIME CLASS

Long:

roughly 30–60 seconds depending on machine.

## RESULT

High-value optional topology change:

- major alternate flank;
- deep industrial shortcut;
- late resource access.

## COUNTERPLAY

Long excavation commitment provides substantial warning.

## COMPETITIVE FREQUENCY

Rare.

More common in campaign maps.

# PART XIII — UNIVERSAL TOPOLOGY RULE

The Phase 01–02 universal-topology rule is now fully resolved.

No essential:

- victory condition;
- mandatory expansion;
- mandatory resource;
- sole attack route

may require Rock Raider excavation.

---

# OPENED ROUTES

Once a Raider excavates a route:

**the route becomes ordinary universal terrain.**

Any compatible enemy or allied ground unit may use it.

Rock Raiders receive no ownership lock.

---

# RE-CLOSING RULE

An excavated opening **cannot be permanently closed again** during standard competitive play.

Players may defend or obstruct it using normal units and permitted buildings.

They cannot rebuild the geological wall.

This produces one clear predictable topology rule.

---

# MIRRORED MATCHUPS

In Raider vs Raider:

- both spawn sectors receive equivalent excavation opportunities;
- equivalent does not require the exact same visual rock shape.

---

# NON-RAIDER MATCHUPS

Excavatable Features may remain present.

Without Rock Raiders they function simply as:

- geological blockers;
- environmental visual language.

No player loses access to an essential part of the map.

---

# RAIDER VS NON-RAIDER

The Raider player may gain:

- optional route efficiency;
- a flank;
- secondary resource value.

The opponent gains:

- scouting opportunity;
- ability to attack excavation;
- eventual use of the opened route.

The map designer must price Raider advantage through:

- time;
- Energy;
- machine commitment;
- openness of the exit.

---

# PART XIV — ASTRONAUT SPATIAL DESIGN

# SERVICE HUBS

Service & Refit Hub coverage remains **18 cells**.

A good forward-service location is:

- strategically useful;
- buildable;
- exposed enough to attack;
- close enough to a combat front to matter.

It is not an inaccessible cliff shelf.

---

# SOLAR EXPLORER

A deployed Solar Explorer requires approximately:

**10 × 10 cells**

of practical clear ground around its deployment center.

The existing Phase 04 10-cell service radius then becomes meaningful without forcing exact pad placement.

---

# FLIGHT OPERATIONS PAD

Base areas supporting Astronaut technology need:

- a 14 × 14 clear operations apron;
- a clean air approach;
- nearby ground circulation.

Decorative cliffs or props may not clip through the pad's operating volume.

---

# RECON DROPSHIP

Competitive unloading requires:

- at least an 8 × 8 pathable ground area;
- terrain connected to the universal ground topology.

A Dropship cannot create a permanent base on a plateau or island that has no normal ground access.

---

# AIR MOBILITY LIMIT

Astronaut aircraft may cross terrain.

They do not erase it because:

- key economic structures remain ground-based;
- most territorial control remains ground-based;
- unloading needs valid terrain;
- anti-air remains available;
- heavy siege remains ground-dependent.

---

# PART XV — ALIEN SPATIAL DESIGN

# COMMAND CORE FOOTHOLDS

Alien expansions are compact, but map expansion pads are **not** reduced in size to match them.

All normal expansion sites retain the same minimum universal buildable area.

The Alien player gains internal layout freedom rather than exclusive access.

---

# RESONANCE VISIBILITY

A Crystal-focused expansion must not contain deep visual cover that completely conceals:

- Resonance Core;
- accompanying Power Couplers

from every practical scout approach.

The structures may be hidden by fog.

They may not be hidden by unfair map geometry once the relevant area is scouted.

Phase 04 already requires Crystal commitment to be visibly represented through lit Resonance slots.

---

# ATTACK VECTORS

Standard maps provide multiple plausible approach directions.

Alien mobility is strongest when it can choose between them.

It must not create routes that only true air can meaningfully contest.

---

# CRYSTAL EXPANSIONS

Crystal field geometry must contain:

- normal ground access;
- anti-air building space nearby;
- room for non-Alien command structures.

A small compact ETX footprint never receives exclusive access.

---

# UNREACHABLE BASE RULE

No standard competitive expansion is:

- air-only;
- cliff-isolated;
- reachable exclusively by Alien craft.

---

# PART XVI — MARTIAN SPATIAL DESIGN

Aero Tubes must feel powerful because of planning rather than because the starting base can connect to everything.

---

# STATION SPACING

Typical efficient Station-to-Station map relationship:

**18–30 build-grid cells.**

This corresponds well to Phase 04's normal Link-cost range.

Long strategic links:

**30–45 cells**

are legal but intentionally expensive.

---

# LINK ROUTING SPACE

Tube Links use map-valid routing space.

The automatic route may cross:

- normal ground;
- Rough Ground;
- shallow liquid;
- modest elevation changes.

Tube Links may not pass directly through:

- solid cliffs;
- major impassable rock;
- air-blocking cavern walls;
- deep voids;
- map boundaries.

The generated path instead bends around them.

Link Ore cost uses the actual generated path length.

---

# VERTICAL DIFFERENCE

A Link may change one standard elevation band where terrain provides sufficient external space for visible Tube supports.

It may not:

- tunnel invisibly through cliffs;
- jump from a low valley to an isolated plateau.

---

# NETWORK CHOICES

Good maps present several options:

### Short efficient links

Cheap and safe but limited in reach.

### Long strategic links

Expensive but connect distant fronts.

### Redundant loops

Increase resilience but require extra Links and Energy.

### Forward nodes

Strong staging positions but exposed.

---

# SAFE-BASE LIMIT

A starting Aero Tube Hangar cannot form a single efficient Link to:

- every expansion;
- the center;
- all side objectives.

On a standard 1v1, important strategic areas are normally at least:

**45–70 routing cells**

from the starting Hangar once actual path geometry is considered.

Martians must extend the network with Settlement Stations.

---

# HEAVY UNITS

The following remain dependent on ordinary ground routes:

- Red Planet Cruiser;
- Recon-Mech RP;
- Red Planet Protector;
- Excavation Searcher.

Phase 04 explicitly keeps them outside Tube transfer.

Therefore every Martian-viable expansion must have conventional heavy ground access.

---

# PART XVII — AIRSPACE & TRUE AIR

Phase 03 establishes true air as a distinct movement layer.

---

# TRUE AIR IGNORES

On ordinary open-surface maps:

- Normal Ground;
- Rough Ground;
- shallow liquid;
- deep liquid;
- ramps;
- ground rubble;
- normal cliffs;
- conventional ground chokepoints.

---

# TRUE AIR IS BLOCKED BY

- playable-map boundary;
- designated Air-Blocking Volume;
- enclosed cavern ceiling;
- massive structural wall;
- orbital/station exterior boundary.

---

# CAVERN AIRSPACE

Caverns have one of two clear states.

## OPEN CAVERN

High enough for true-air operation.

Aircraft behave normally within the chamber.

## LOW TUNNEL

Marked by visibly low ceiling/rock framing.

True-air movement is prohibited.

There is no ambiguous invisible ceiling.

---

# LARGE AIRCRAFT

Large units such as:

- Tunnel Transport;
- MX-81 Operations Aircraft;
- Alien Mothership

use larger avoidance radii.

They cannot visually clip through narrow rock arches or tight station corridors even where a Mono Jet might fit.

Maps with enclosed air routes therefore author sufficient clearance explicitly.

---

# LARGE STRUCTURES

Aircraft route around large Air-Blocking structures and map geometry.

Normal player-built structures do not create arbitrary permanent air walls.

---

# CLIFFS

Surface cliffs do not block true air.

This preserves the meaning of aircraft.

---

# ANTI-AIR SIGHT

Surface cliffs do not automatically hide aircraft passing overhead.

Cavern walls and true Air-Blocking Volumes do occlude sight where appropriate.

---

# WHY AIR DOES NOT INVALIDATE THE MAP

Phase 03 already establishes that aircraft:

- cannot mine;
- cannot normally construct bases;
- cannot independently hold most objectives;
- require Energy and infrastructure;
- have limited true-air siege;
- remain vulnerable to dedicated anti-air.

Phase 05 adds:

- ground-only resource control;
- ground-connected competitive expansions;
- unloading restrictions;
- cavern airspace constraints;
- map-authorable Air-Blocking Volumes.

**6–9 pathable cells**

Use:

- small secondary routes;
- defensible side access;
- infantry/light-machine interaction.

Never the only main army connection between major sectors.

---

**10–15 cells**

The most common strategic choke.

Supports:

- normal army movement;
- defenses;
- siege interaction.

**16–24 cells**

Supports:

- medium-scale formations;
- flanking within the lane;
- heavy machinery.

---

**25+ cells**

Used for:

- maneuver battles;
- multiple firing lines;
- large machine readability;
- air-ground combined arms.

---

# HEAVY UNIT STANDARD

Every standard competitive map must provide at least one continuous major army route with:

**12 cells minimum clear width**

between each main strategic region.

Major combat spaces should frequently reach:

**16–24 cells.**

---

# LARGE MACHINE OPERATIONS

Maps must support readable use of:

- Chrome Crusher;
- Tunnel Transport cargo operations;
- MT-201 Ultra-Drill Walker;

Ultra-Drill deployment and Tunnel Transport unloading require deliberately clear operating zones.

# PART XX — DESTRUCTIBLE TERRAIN

Destructible Terrain is distinct from Raider Excavation.

---

# EXCAVATABLE

- Raider engineering interaction;
- authored geological barrier;
- opened through mining tools;
- permanently opens once excavated.

---

# DESTRUCTIBLE

- any faction may attack;
- uses normal combat damage;
- may remove an obstacle or cover object.

Examples:

- abandoned machinery;
- weak industrial doors;
- fragile crystal spires;
- ruined barriers.

---

# HAZARD-TRIGGERABLE

Environmental object whose destruction or activation produces a telegraphed hazard.

Example:

A charged crystal formation discharges into a marked nearby zone when destroyed.

---

# COMPETITIVE RULES

A standard destructible object may:

- open a minor lane;
- remove line-of-sight cover;
- clear build space.

It may **not** destroy the sole universal route between critical map regions.

Competitive bridges that carry essential topology are not normally destructible.

Once a destructible obstacle is destroyed, its resulting state remains predictable.

---

# PART XXI — ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARDS

Standard ranked maps use hazards sparingly.

Most contain:

- zero major hazards;
- or one mild predictable hazard family.

Hazard-heavy maps belong to specialized multiplayer or campaign.

---

# 1. LAVA / THERMAL POOL

## TELEGRAPH

Permanent bright liquid boundary.

## EFFECT

Ground units cannot enter.

Air crosses normally.

## FREQUENCY

Common in volcanic biomes.

## RANDOMNESS

None.

## COUNTERPLAY

Route planning.

## FACTION INTERACTION

No immunity.

---

# 2. VOLCANIC VENT

## TELEGRAPH

Glow, shaking, steam, increasing heat before eruption.

## EFFECT

Temporary damage/area denial.

## FREQUENCY

Moderate on volcanic special maps.

## RANDOMNESS

Fixed cycle.

## COUNTERPLAY

Move before eruption.

---

# 3. CRYSTAL DISCHARGE

## TELEGRAPH

Crystal illumination builds before discharge.

## EFFECT

Temporary localized energetic damage and sensor interference.

## FREQUENCY

Low–moderate.

## RANDOMNESS

Fixed or map-scripted cycle.

## COUNTERPLAY

Wait, reroute, or fight between pulses.

Aliens are not immune.

---

# 4. ROCKFALL ZONE

## TELEGRAPH

Dust, small falling debris, visible cracks.

## EFFECT

Temporary ground danger and short-lived rubble.

## FREQUENCY

Rare in standard multiplayer.

## RANDOMNESS

Predictable trigger/cycle.

## COUNTERPLAY

Leave marked zone.

---

# 5. ICE FRACTURE

## TELEGRAPH

Bright crack lines spread over several seconds.

## EFFECT

Temporarily turns a region into Rough Ground or closes a clearly marked specialized route.

## FREQUENCY

Specialized maps.

## RANDOMNESS

Predictable.

## COUNTERPLAY

Route adaptation.

---

# 6. PRESSURE / STEAM VENT

## TELEGRAPH

Visible vent and pressure build-up.

## EFFECT

Short local damage/vision obstruction.

## FREQUENCY

Cavern maps.

## RANDOMNESS

Cyclical.

## COUNTERPLAY

Time passage.

---

# 7. ION STORM

## TELEGRAPH

Distant electrical buildup and clear UI/world warning.

## EFFECT

On special maps only:

temporarily reduces true-air sight within the storm zone.

It does **not** alter faction Energy generation.

## FREQUENCY

Rare.

## RANDOMNESS

Predictable route/cycle.

## COUNTERPLAY

Delay flight or reroute.

---

# 8. ABANDONED MACHINERY

## TELEGRAPH

Visible moving machinery cycle.

## EFFECT

Mechanical crush, sweep, or access denial.

## FREQUENCY

Campaign and specialized industrial maps.

## RANDOMNESS

Fixed mechanical rhythm.

## COUNTERPLAY

Observe and cross during safe phase or destroy where permitted.

---

# PART XXII — ROCK MONSTERS

Phase 03 establishes Rock Monsters as neutral hostile creatures and resource guardians.

---

# HABITAT

Rock Monsters are associated with:

- deep caverns;
- mineralized geology;
- rich Ore regions;
- crystal-bearing formations.

---

# ACTIVATION

A competitive Rock Monster normally begins near a visibly marked habitat.

It activates when:

- a unit enters approximately 8 cells;
- or nearby mining/excavation begins.

---

# AGGRESSION

Rock Monsters attack:

1. nearby units threatening the habitat;
2. workers actively harvesting nearby resources;
3. structures built inside their defended area.

They do not hunt across the entire map.

---

# PATROL

Competitive Rock Monsters use:

- stationary guarding;
- or short fixed patrols.

No unpredictable global wandering.

---

# LEASH

If pulled approximately 12–16 cells from its habitat, a Monster returns.

---

# RESPAWN

No competitive respawn.

Campaign missions may explicitly script additional creatures.

---

# REWARD

There is no default Warcraft-style bounty.

The reward for dealing with a Monster is:

- access to the guarded resource;
- access to the guarded position.

Specific campaign missions may add scenario rewards.

---

# COMPETITIVE FREQUENCY

Standard ranked map:

**normally 0.**

Specialized competitive map:

**0–2.**

A Rock Monster should be a map-specific event rather than universal creep-camp infrastructure.

Rock Raiders receive no immunity.

---

# PART XXIII — OTHER NEUTRAL LIFE / ENVIRONMENTAL ENTITIES

The game does not require a large neutral bestiary.

Two broad ambient categories are permitted as:

# NEW GAME CONTENT

## FRONTIER BURROWERS

Small non-combat subterranean or rocky-world creatures.

Behavior:

- emerge briefly;
- flee from vehicles;
- do not attack;
- do not affect pathing.

## HIGH-ATMOSPHERE GLIDERS

Small distant flying lifeforms on appropriate worlds.

Behavior:

- ambient only;
- never targetable during competitive play.

These families improve world believability without becoming gameplay systems.

No additional normal neutral combat creature family is introduced in Phase 05.

---

# PART XXIV — NEUTRAL STRUCTURES & MAP OBJECTS

Neutral structures are categorized as:

## TERRAIN-ONLY

Examples:

- crashed craft;
- abandoned stations;
- mining equipment;
- Martian civic machinery.

No interaction beyond collision/readability.

## DESTRUCTIBLE

Examples:

- industrial door;
- ruined barrier;
- old machine blocking a route.

## REPAIRABLE / ACTIVATABLE

Reserved primarily for campaign and explicitly labeled special multiplayer scenarios.

Examples:

- disabled bridge;
- observation system;
- old processor.

## CAPTURABLE

Not part of the standard ranked-map ruleset.

Campaign maps may use capture.

---

# STANDARD COMPETITIVE POLICY

Neutral interactables are sparse.

The default ranked map does not contain powerful neutral structures granting permanent economic or combat bonuses.

Terrain and faction systems remain the primary source of strategic depth.

---

# PART XXV — ABANDONED / ANCIENT LOCATIONS

Most ruins in the Reach have understandable frontier origins:

- previous human expeditions;
- Rock Raider work sites;
- old Martian settlements;
- abandoned Alien operations;
- independent industrial infrastructure.

The term **ancient** is reserved for a much smaller category.

Some sites genuinely appear older than current known frontier activity.

Their origin is **not resolved**.

They may include:

- geometric buried structures;
- unexplained foundations;
- unusual but inactive machinery.

Phase 05 does not establish a hidden fifth civilization.

Ancient material provides:

- adventure;
- mystery;
- campaign texture.

It does not dominate the world premise.

---

# PART XXVI — BASE-BUILDING SPACE

# STANDARD MAIN

Practical footprint:

- 42 × 42-cell operating area;
- minimum 34 × 34 strongly buildable core.

# STANDARD EXPANSION

Minimum useful buildable footprint:

**34 × 34 cells.**

---

# CLIFF CLEARANCE

Ordinary buildings require approximately:

**1 cell** clear from hard collision edges.

Large production/command structures require approximately:

**2 cells** from cliff edges and heavy obstacles.

---

# RESOURCE CLEARANCE

A **6-cell resource-service apron** around active deposit access faces remains unbuildable.

This prevents:

- worker traps;
- accidental deposit sealing;
- exploitative defensive construction.

---

# DECORATION

Most small decorative geology:

- has no collision;
- does not block construction.

Only clearly readable strategic objects receive gameplay collision.

A beautiful map may not become frustrating because dozens of small rocks invalidate building placement.

---

# HARRASSMENT ACCESS

Base footprints must provide at least one attackable edge.

No main or expansion can be completely sealed by decorative geography.

---

# PART XXVII — MAP READABILITY

At normal RTS camera distance, the world must communicate gameplay before UI icons are consulted.

---

# WALKABLE TERRAIN

Uses visually continuous surfaces and clear navigable width.

---

# CLIFFS

Use:

- unmistakable vertical face;
- strong edge silhouette;
- no ambiguous shallow decorative slopes that look traversable.

---

# RAMPS

Use:

- widened entry;
- consistent slope;
- distinct edge treatment.

---

# BUILDABLE AREAS

Read as:

- stable;
- relatively level;
- less cluttered.

A build-grid may appear during placement but is not required for ordinary interpretation.

---

# ORE

Uses:

- bulky mineral formations;
- clear depletion states.

---

# CRYSTALS

Use:

- distinct luminous geometry;
- readable cluster density;
- visible depletion one major piece at a time.

---

# HAZARDS

Must telegraph through the environment before damage occurs.

---

# EXCAVATABLE FEATURES

All five classes use a consistent authored geological language.

A player should never confuse a drillable wall with decorative background cliff.

---

# TUBE LINKS

Tube routes remain clearly visible above ground and have visible Station endpoints.

---

# WATER

Shallow and deep liquid use visibly different:

- depth;
- edge treatment;
- surface value.

---

# AIR-BLOCKED CAVES

Low ceilings and framing communicate prohibited airspace directly.

---

# DESTRUCTIBLES

Use visible structural weakness:

- cracked supports;
- exposed mechanisms;
- damaged frame.

---

# PART XXVIII — FOG OF WAR & TERRAIN DISCOVERY

The game uses one shared fundamental fog framework.

---

# UNEXPLORED

Exact:

- terrain;
- resources;
- neutral objects

remain hidden.

The player knows the coarse outer map silhouette from the mission/map briefing but not every internal feature.

---

# EXPLORED

Static terrain remains remembered.

Previously explored areas become dimmed but readable.

---

# LAST-KNOWN STRUCTURES

Previously scouted enemy structures remain as desaturated last-known silhouettes until:

- rescouted;
- contradicted by new information.

They are clearly distinguishable from confirmed current structures.

---

# ELEVATION

Cliffs occlude ground sight.

High ground grants the modest sight advantage defined earlier.

---

# CAVERNS

Vision does not pass through:

- solid cavern walls;
- sealed tunnels;
- low-ceiling rock volume.

---

# LARGE ENVIRONMENTAL OBJECTS

Previously explored static formations remain on the remembered map.

---

# TERRAIN TRANSFORMATION

The strongest competitive rule is:

# TERRAIN CHANGES DO NOT UPDATE ENEMY MEMORY OUTSIDE VISION.

If Rock Raiders excavate a wall while the opponent cannot see it:

- the opponent's remembered map continues showing the sealed wall;
- the new route is not automatically revealed;
- enemy pathfinding based on remembered information does not automatically exploit it.

Once allied vision sees the opening:

- remembered topology updates permanently.

The same rule applies to:

- destructible obstacles;
- destroyed neutral machinery;
- other authored dynamic terrain.

This makes scouting strategically meaningful.

---

# PART XXIX — MAP ARCHETYPES

Biomes and archetypes are independent.

A volcanic world can use the same strategic archetype as a frozen moon.

---

# 1. OPEN FRONTIER

## PREMISE

Large maneuver spaces separated by moderate ridges.

## EXPANSIONS

Wide lateral distribution.

## RESOURCES

No central monopoly.

## ROUTES

Several broad lanes.

## CRYSTALS

Often lateral.

## EXCAVATION

Low–moderate.

## AIR

Useful but exposed.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Many viable geometries.

## STRENGTH

Excellent combined-arms battles.

## RISK

Can become shapeless if terrain landmarks are too weak.

---

# 2. DUAL FRONT

## PREMISE

Two major parallel attack fronts connect the players.

## EXPANSIONS

Distributed along both fronts.

## RESOURCES

One lane may be Ore-rich while the other offers Crystals.

## EXCAVATION

Can connect fronts.

## AIR

Useful for switching lanes.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Strong network choice.

## RISK

One front must not become obviously superior.

---

# 3. BROKEN RING

## PREMISE

Main travel forms a partial loop around difficult central terrain.

## EXPANSIONS

Distributed around ring sectors.

## CRYSTALS

Central-side pockets or outer ring.

## EXCAVATION

May create radial shortcuts.

## AIR

Can cross central terrain but remains vulnerable.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Choice between ring links and cross-links.

## RISK

Travel times can become excessive.

---

# 4. CROSSROADS BASIN

## PREMISE

Several broad routes intersect in a central basin.

## EXPANSIONS

Mostly outside exact center.

## CRYSTALS

One or more central-side fields.

## EXCAVATION

Optional side connections.

## AIR

High.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Forward Station is powerful but exposed.

## RISK

Do not stack every valuable resource in the crossing.

---

# 5. TERRACED VALLEYS

## PREMISE

High and low zones alternate across the map.

## EXPANSIONS

Mix of valley and high-ground sites.

## ROUTES

Broad ramps.

## CRYSTALS

Often in exposed terraces.

## EXCAVATION

May bypass one elevation transition.

## AIR

Useful for route switching.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Links must respect vertical path geometry.

## RISK

High ground cannot become one-ramp fortress territory.

---

# 6. CAVERN BRANCHES

## PREMISE

Several large chambers connected by broad tunnel branches.

## EXPANSIONS

One or two chambers per side plus contested junctions.

## EXCAVATION

High.

## AIR

Restricted to major chambers.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Tube routes bend through chamber topology.

## RISK

Avoid corridor-maze gameplay.

---

# 7. PERIMETER RICHES

## PREMISE

The highest-value late resources sit away from the center.

## EXPANSIONS

Edge/side focused.

## ROUTES

Center provides transit rather than monopoly.

## CRYSTALS

Lateral fields.

## EXCAVATION

May shorten side-to-center travel.

## AIR

Strong for harassment.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Encourages long strategic branches.

## RISK

Peripheral economy must remain contestable.

---

# 8. DIVIDED CENTER

## PREMISE

A large impassable central feature separates two or three major crossings.

## EXPANSIONS

Distributed around crossings.

## CRYSTALS

Near but not directly inside crossings.

## EXCAVATION

Optional minor cut through portions of divide.

## AIR

Can cross the divider but ground objectives force continued route control.

## RISK

Ground distance must not become excessively long.

---

# 9. CAUSEWAY PLATFORM

## PREMISE

Large connected platforms separated by non-ground space or deep terrain.

## EXPANSIONS

Each platform remains ground-connected through universal causeways.

## CRYSTALS

Exposed platform edges.

## EXCAVATION

Low.

## AIR

Above-average strategic relevance.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Tube routing follows causeway geography.

## RISK

Must not become an air-only map.

---

# 10. LATTICE FRONTIER

## PREMISE

A network of broad connected lanes with several triangular relationships.

## EXPANSIONS

Frequent intermediate positions.

## CRYSTALS

At network junctions and side branches.

## EXCAVATION

Can add one or two new links.

## AIR

Moderate.

## MARTIAN NETWORK

Many choices but no perfect all-map hub.

## RISK

Too many connections can reduce positional meaning.

# PART XXX — COMPETITIVE MAP STANDARD

Every standard 1v1 map must pass the following checklist.

---

## ECONOMY

- approximately 1,800 safe starting Ore per player;
- no starting Crystal field;
- natural available before main depletion;
- late contested resources exist.

## EXPANSION

- at least three meaningful post-main expansion decisions per player on standard maps;
- natural not completely free;
- at least one risky economic option.

## PATHING

- one 12+ cell primary heavy route;
- no mandatory one-unit-width funnels;
- all normal expansions heavy-ground accessible.

## SCOUTING

- multiple informative scout routes;
- no unscoutable economic pocket;
- Resonance infrastructure and Tube networks can be observed through normal map control.

## AIR

- air changes approach geometry;
- air cannot establish exclusive bases;
- unloading zones remain ground-connected;
- no inaccessible harassment pocket.

## BUILDING SPACE

- 42 × 42 usable main operating zone;
- 34 × 34 expansion site;
- enough room for all faction infrastructure.

## SIEGE

- defended bases have meaningful siege approach space;
- siege is not forced into one tiny corridor.

## FACTION ASYMMETRY

- faction systems matter;
- no system decides the map automatically.

## ROCK RAIDER EXCAVATION

- optional value only;
- clear counterplay;
- no mandatory Raider access.

## ASTRONAUT SERVICE

- forward service can matter;
- Service Hub positions remain vulnerable.

## ALIEN PRESSURE

- multiple vectors exist;
- Crystal commitments remain contestable.

## MARTIAN TUBES

- useful node geometry;
- network cannot cover whole map from main;
- heavy units retain ground routes.

## LATE GAME

- Deep Ore or remaining contested resources pull armies away from fully safe bases.

## VISUAL READABILITY

- ramps, cliffs, resources, hazards, dynamic terrain and paths readable from strategic camera.

A standard 1v1 must function for all **16 ordered faction-side combinations**, including mirrors.

# PART XXXI — SPAWN FAIRNESS

Ranked competitive maps primarily use:

# ROTATIONAL STRATEGIC SYMMETRY

For 1v1 this normally means **180-degree rotational symmetry**.

This is preferred over strict mirror symmetry because it:

- avoids visual artificiality;
- supports organic geology;
- prevents directional-left/right faction biases;
- preserves exact strategic equivalence.

---

# EQUALITY REQUIREMENTS

## RESOURCES

Exact equivalent resource value.

## TRAVEL

Equivalent major travel distance within approximately **±3%**.

## EXCAVATION

Equivalent:

- count;
- Energy class;
- strategic value.

Exact rock shapes may differ.

## CRYSTALS

Equivalent access and total value.

## HIGH GROUND

Equivalent defensive and scouting opportunities.

## TUBE GEOMETRY

Equivalent Station-to-Station useful distances.

## AIR ROUTES

Equivalent effective access to main and expansion airspace.

---

# AUTHORED ASYMMETRY

Small visual and geometric asymmetries are allowed inside rotationally equivalent regions.

Ranked maps do not use strategically unequal spawn positions.

Campaign maps are exempt.

---

# PART XXXII — SIX MATCHUP MAP TEST

| Matchup | Principal map risk | Authoring safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| **Rock Raiders vs Astronauts** | Raider excavation creates unanswerable shortcut or Astronaut air endlessly bypasses slow Raider ground force | Excavated exits are scoutable and shared; air-only economic pockets forbidden; Raider heavy routes remain broad |
| **Rock Raiders vs Aliens** | Alien early Crystal pressure outpaces Worksite maturation | First large Crystal fields exposed; no safe Alien-side rich field; Raider natural receives viable defensive geometry |
| **Rock Raiders vs Martians** | Tube network outmaneuvers every Industrial Advance | Major objectives not arranged on one perfect Station chain; Tube exits exposed; Raider excavation can create alternate shared routes |
| **Astronauts vs Aliens** | Too many safe air approaches make defense impossible | Bases have anti-air staging space; air cannot own expansions; Alien Resonance investment must be scoutable |
| **Astronauts vs Martians** | Astronaut mobility or Martian prepared networks make positional commitment irrelevant | Heavy objectives remain ground-centered; Martian network has exposed nodes; Astronaut service requires vulnerable forward infrastructure |
| **Aliens vs Martians** | Alien Surge always reaches network weak points before Martians react, or Tube reinforcement negates Alien attack windows | Multiple but finite approaches; Tube transit remains real time; no protected central Station; Crystal expansions expose Alien commitments |

# PART XXXIII — TEAM MAPS

Team maps increase strategic fronts rather than simply scaling 1v1 outward.

---

# ALLIED BASE SPACING

Typical allied main centers:

**30–45 cells apart.**

Allies are close enough to cooperate but cannot completely overlap infrastructure.

---

# SHARED FRONT

Each team normally possesses:

- one broad shared pressure axis;
- at least one secondary flank.

---

# EXPANSION OWNERSHIP

Players receive plausible personal naturals.

Later expansions increasingly sit in:

- shared team territory;
- contested middle territory.

No map preassigns every expansion permanently.

---

# CRYSTALS

Major Crystal fields are placed where teammates must decide:

- which player invests;
- how the team protects the position.

They do not spawn immediately behind one player's main.

---

# FACTION SYSTEM OWNERSHIP RULE

Faction systems remain **player-owned**.

One player's infrastructure does not merge with another player's system.

Specifically:

- Martian Tube Networks remain separate between allied Martian players;
- Rock Raider Worksites do not share Worksite service or resource routing with allied Raiders;
- Astronaut Service networks do not provide allied Mission Refit;
- Alien Charge and Resonance Cores are owner-specific.

Normal later combat/support rules may allow explicit allied assistance where separately designed.

Macro networks never merge automatically.

---

# AIR LANES

Team maps provide multiple air vectors but do not permit direct unopposed flight from one main to another without passing through defensible space.

---

# CHOKE DENSITY

Team maps use fewer small chokes and more:

- medium passages;
- broad lanes;
- open combat zones.

Large multi-player armies need room to remain readable.

---

# 3v3

3v3 is the largest core competitive team format.

Its maps emphasize:

- several simultaneous fronts;
- decentralized Crystal objectives;
- enough expansions for six economies;
- strong anti-snowball route redundancy.

---

# PART XXXVII — ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING

The battlefield should look as though the frontier existed before the match began.

Useful storytelling elements include:

- abandoned mining marks;
- worn excavation cuts;
- inactive Tube segments;
- crashed craft;
- partial bases;
- drill scars;
- old resource containers;
- Martian civic mechanisms;
- failed survey camps;
- sealed tunnel entrances.

---

# READABILITY RULE

Environmental storytelling may not:

- narrow a path unexpectedly;
- conceal resources;
- look like active faction infrastructure when it is not;
- create large numbers of collision props.

Strategic readability overrides decorative density.

# PART XXXVIII — LEGO ENVIRONMENT PHILOSOPHY

The environment exists as a coherent LEGO-designed universe.

It is not a tabletop diorama.

---

# GEOLOGY

Large rock masses may use:

- simplified faceted construction;
- LEGO-compatible angular forms;
- layered shapes;
- selective constructed geometry.

They do not need visible studs on every square meter.

---

# CONSTRUCTED OBJECTS

Machines and structures use unmistakable LEGO engineering logic:

- modular joints;
- beams;
- panels;
- hinges;
- tubes;
- visible assemblies.

---

# SCALE

Terrain is not required to be literally buildable from commercially available LEGO pieces at physical toy scale.

Strategic readability and game-world coherence take priority.

---

# SURFACES

Avoid:

- macro plastic photography;
- giant fingerprints;
- toy scratches;
- cardboard scenery;
- visible physical baseplates everywhere.

---

# STUDS

Studs may appear selectively on:

- constructed platforms;
- machine surfaces;
- intentional modular attachment points.

They are not a universal terrain texture.

# PART XLIII — MAP DESIGN ANTI-PATTERNS

The following are prohibited for normal competitive maps.

1. **Huge empty rectangles** with no strategic terrain relationships.

2. **Corridor mazes** in which medium-scale armies cannot maneuver.

3. **Air-bypass maps** where true air reaches every economy while ground forces require extreme detours.

4. **Air-only expansions.**

5. **Raider-exclusive resources or objectives** behind Excavatable Terrain.

6. **Excavation gimmick maps** where nearly every route begins sealed.

7. **One perfect Martian Tube spine** connecting main, natural, center, and every late expansion.

8. **Safe uncontested Alien Crystal fields** directly behind the Alien spawn side.

9. **Astronaut-cramped mains** where Flight Pads or Service infrastructure cannot fit.

10. **Heavy-unit-invalid maps** whose key lanes exclude Chrome Crusher, MT-201 operations, or Excavation Searcher.

11. **Tiny single-ramp expansion fortresses.**

12. **Crystal + Deep Ore super-centers** on every map.

13. **Decorative collision clutter** that makes building placement unpredictable.

14. **Hidden terrain rules** visible only through UI icons.

15. **Random lethal hazards.**

16. **Neutral creep-camp maps** where clearing creatures becomes mandatory economic progression.

17. **Symmetry without choice** where every expansion and attack path is an identical straight line.

18. **Perfectly safe forward-service shelves** reachable by Astronaut aircraft but not enemy ground forces.

19. **Unscoutable Resonance pockets.**

20. **Tube-only Martian heavy mobility assumptions** despite heavy units being excluded from Tube transfer.

21. **Permanent destructible last-route closures.**

22. **Energy-biome bonuses** that quietly modify faction generators on standard maps.

23. **Resource abundance that makes expansion optional.**

24. **Late maps with no economically meaningful contested territory.**

# PART XLVIII — PHASE 06 REQUIREMENTS

The next phase is:

# COMBAT, DAMAGE & BALANCE FRAMEWORK

Phase 06 inherits the following world constraints as mandatory combat context.

---

# ENGAGEMENT SPACE

Standard combat occurs across:

- 10–15-cell medium passages;
- 16–24-cell broad lanes;
- 25+ cell open fields.

Major heavy army routes are never narrower than approximately 12 cells.

Phase 06 should therefore balance:

- frontage;
- splash behavior;
- siege;
- formation density

for these spaces.

---

# MAP DISTANCE

Standard 1v1:

- 105–120 cells main-to-main ground distance;
- 20–26 cells main-to-natural;
- approximately 80–100 seconds representative main-army cross-map travel.

Combat mobility values should preserve this intended strategic travel scale.

---

# ELEVATION

Phase 06 must assume:

- high ground improves visibility/clear sightlines;
- no inherent accuracy bonus;
- no inherent attack-range bonus;
- cliffs occlude ground line of sight.

Weapon-specific exceptions, such as legitimate indirect fire, may be designed in Phase 06 but must remain readable.

---

# GROUND MOVEMENT

Phase 06 inherits:

- Foot;
- Wheeled;
- Tracked;
- Walker;
- Ground Hover;
- Amphibious;
- True Air.

It should not create hidden terrain multipliers that contradict the Phase 05 compatibility table.

---

# AIR

True air:

- ignores normal ground routes;
- cannot independently replace surface economy/control;
- can be constrained by Air-Blocking Volumes;
- remains subject to anti-air.

Air weapon and AA ranges must therefore be balanced around meaningful approach and retreat geometry rather than unlimited straight-line dominance.

---

# SIEGE

Siege must function in:

- 12+ cell approaches;
- broad base edges;
- 34 × 34 expansion footprints.

It must not assume every base can only be attacked through one choke.

---

# EXPANSION VULNERABILITY

Naturals are closer but still attackable.

Forward, Crystal, Deep Ore, and Risk expansions intentionally expose economic value.

Phase 06 should treat economic raiding as strategically meaningful without assuming workers always operate inside perfect defensive funnels.

---

# RETREAT GEOMETRY

Maps contain:

- broad retreat lanes;
- ramps;
- side paths;
- medium chokes.

Movement speeds, pursuit, slows, displacement, and range must preserve the possibility of:

- retreat;
- interception;
- screening.

---

# HAZARDS

Environmental hazards are:

- predictable;
- mostly avoidable;
- not faction-immunity systems.

Phase 06 can define how combat damage interacts with them but must not make hazards the primary damage source in standard maps.

---

# ROCK RAIDER EXCAVATION

Combat balance must assume Raiders can sometimes:

- open optional flank routes;
- shorten reinforcement/logistics paths.

Those routes become usable by everyone.

Raider units therefore do not need extra combat power simply because excavation exists.

---

# ASTRONAUT MOBILITY

Combat balance must account for:

- true-air presence;
- Dropship transport;
- Switch Fighter transformation;
- forward servicing.

However, Service Hubs and deployment areas remain vulnerable map commitments.

---

# ALIEN PRESSURE

Aliens receive:

- multiple attack-vector opportunities;
- Crystal-contested map geography;
- space for Resonance footholds.

Phase 06 should balance Surge combat knowing that map design already prevents uncontestable Crystal safety and unscoutable Cores.

---

# MARTIAN NETWORK MOBILITY

Phase 06 must assume:

- Tube transfer has actual travel time;
- only light eligible assets use Tubes;
- exits are predictable;
- heavy Martian armies use normal ground routes.

Combat statistics therefore should not treat the entire Martian army as teleport-capable.

---

# PHASE 06 BOUNDARY

Phase 05 does **not** define:

- hit points;
- armor values;
- damage;
- DPS;
- weapon cooldown;
- exact attack range;
- projectile speed;
- final movement speed;
- splash radius;
- accuracy;
- repair rates;
- numerical counter ratios.

Those now belong to Phase 06.
