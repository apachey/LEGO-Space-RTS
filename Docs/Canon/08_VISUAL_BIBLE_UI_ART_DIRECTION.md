# PART I — VISUAL NORTH STAR

# PREMIUM LEGO SCIENCE-FICTION MACHINERY,
# BUILT TO BE READ FROM AN RTS CAMERA,
# NOT PHOTOGRAPHED AS TOYS.

The world obeys LEGO construction logic but exists as a living stylized universe.

The visual hierarchy is:

**function → faction → silhouette → motion → material → detail.**

Eleven canonical pillars govern all production.

### 1. LEGO construction is structural

Parts appear attached, hinged, clipped, pinned, tracked, wheeled or articulated for an understandable reason.

### 2. Silhouette carries gameplay

No important unit depends on decals to communicate its role.

### 3. Factions remain visually independent

A neutral-gray silhouette pass must still reveal strong faction family identity.

### 4. Machinery explains itself

A drill looks capable of drilling.

A loader visibly scoops.

A Tube visibly transports.

A transforming craft visibly possesses joints that permit transformation.

### 5. Stylization serves clarity

Important forms may be enlarged or compressed, but source identity is never sacrificed for spectacle.

### 6. Color is structural

Large color masses identify source family and machine sections.

Color is not noisy surface decoration.

### 7. Materials feel LEGO-derived, not photographed

Polymer, transparent elements, rubber, metal tools and crystal materials are stylized game materials.

### 8. Animation reveals construction

Movement should constantly remind the player that machines are built from connected physical mechanisms.

### 9. Environment supports the army

Terrain is rich enough to feel premium but deliberately lower in strategic contrast than playable assets.

### 10. VFX reveal cause and function

A weapon effect should help explain what the machine is doing.

### 11. UI belongs to the universe without becoming a toy dashboard

It is clean RTS instrumentation with faction-specific mechanical character.

# PART II — WHAT “LEGO” MEANS VISUALLY

LEGO identity comes from recognizable construction relationships.

Essential visual grammar includes:

- bricks;
- plates;
- tiles;
- slopes;
- wedges;
- studs;
- clips;
- bars;
- hinges;
- Technic pins and axles;
- wheels;
- tracks;
- articulated joints;
- transparent canopies;
- pneumatic/tube pieces;
- minifigure geometry;
- printed elements;
- detachable modules;
- physical transformation;
- visible connection logic.

A model may contain custom game-resolution geometry where required, but that geometry must look as though it could have been achieved through credible LEGO component design.

LEGO identity does **not** require:

- exposed studs on every surface;
- visible mold seams;
- fingerprints;
- microscopic scratches;
- physically accurate tabletop scale;
- shallow photographic depth of field;
- toy-box lighting;
- plastic macro photography.

# PART III — CONSTRUCTION LOGIC

Every production asset is divided into three geometric importance bands.

## HERO GEOMETRY

Source-defining geometry.

Examples:

- Chrome Crusher drill;
- Granite Grinder leg/drill relationship;
- T3-Trike three-wheel arrangement;
- Solar Explorer solar panels;
- Switch Fighter transforming chassis;
- ETX Infiltrator transformation frame;
- Alien Mothership radial/segmented hull;
- Recon-Mech body/leg silhouette;
- Aero Tube pump and Tube geometry.

Hero geometry survives every gameplay LOD.

## SUPPORT GEOMETRY

Geometry that establishes convincing LEGO construction.

Examples:

- hinges;
- beams;
- plate stacking;
- wheel mounts;
- cockpit framing;
- clamps;
- machinery cages;
- Tube supports;
- panel attachment.

Support geometry remains visible at normal combat zoom.

## MICRO DETAIL

Close-view enrichment.

Examples:

- underside studs;
- very small clips;
- control levers;
- individual hose couplers;
- tiny vents;
- printed panel details;
- small hand tools.

Micro detail may collapse aggressively at normal/far LOD.

The source-fidelity preservation order is:

1. silhouette;
2. major proportions;
3. cockpit/canopy;
4. locomotion;
5. source-defining mechanism;
6. dominant color blocking;
7. important transparent elements;
8. secondary construction detail;
9. tertiary decoration.

# PART IV — STUD POLICY

Studs function as **construction punctuation**, not surface noise.

## Encouraged

Higher visible stud density is appropriate on:

- Rock Raider work decks;
- industrial loading areas;
- exposed Martian mechanical platforms;
- Life on Mars Field Systems;
- modular connection zones;
- unfinished/construction states;
- machinery service surfaces;
- terrain interactables intended to feel constructible.

## Reduced

Lower visible stud density is appropriate on:

- Mars Mission aerospace hulls;
- large Mission Systems body panels;
- Alien curved craft shells;
- aerodynamic canopies;
- large faction-color surfaces requiring clear visual blocks.

## Baseline density

At the normal gameplay view:

- Raiders: medium-high;
- Astronaut Field Systems: medium;
- Astronaut Mission Systems: low-medium;
- Aliens: low-medium on hulls, higher at mechanisms;
- Martians: medium-high at open mechanical assemblies.

No asset may be completely smoothed into generic science fiction.

No asset may become a noisy carpet of studs.

# PART V — MATERIAL PHILOSOPHY

The renderer uses a coherent stylized PBR family.

## Molded polymer

Primary LEGO-like material.

Baseline perceptual roughness:

**approximately 0.32–0.50.**

It produces:

- broad controlled highlights;
- readable curvature;
- clear color;
- no mirror-like showroom reflections.

Black polymer is lifted enough in ambient response to preserve construction.

## Tool metal

Used selectively on:

- drills;
- cutters;
- exposed axles;
- industrial teeth;
- mechanical contact surfaces.

Baseline roughness:

**approximately 0.22–0.40.**

Chrome-source parts may appear brighter and more metallic but never behave as perfect mirrors.

## Rubber

Tires and source-supported flexible elements use a softer, higher-roughness material.

Rubber remains clearly different from black polymer.

## Transparent polymer

Used for:

- canopies;
- windscreens;
- Tubes;
- lamps;
- crystal housings.

Transparency is strongly tinted and stylized.

Expensive optical refraction is minimized.

## Crystal

Crystals use:

- transparent/faceted body;
- bright internal value;
- restrained emissive core;
- controlled edge bloom.

The geometry remains readable when bloom is disabled.

## Edge response

Broad highlights reveal LEGO construction.

Tiny photographed plastic imperfections do not.

# PART VI — WEAR & WEATHERING

Assets exist in an active frontier.

Wear is localized and functional.

Allowed:

- fine dust accumulation;
- shallow scuff marks near contact zones;
- abrasion on drill/cutter tips;
- soot near heavy Raider machinery;
- residue around volcanic operation;
- light frost on cold-environment equipment;
- dust at wheel wells;
- panel-edge wear after damage.

Forbidden:

- full-body realistic military grime;
- rust covering faction colors;
- abandoned-vehicle weathering on functional armies;
- heavy oil streaks;
- photoreal chipped paint systems.

The canonical principle is:

# USED LEGO MACHINERY, NOT REALISTIC AGED STEEL.

# PART VII — EDGES & BEVELS

Visible hard-poly edges receive subtle premium-game bevels.

The bevel is:

- sufficient to catch normal gameplay lighting;
- small relative to the LEGO part;
- never large enough to distort the identity of slopes, bricks or tiles.

Hero parts preserve recognizable planar proportions.

Rounded bevels cannot turn:

- bricks into soft blocks;
- slopes into inflated toys;
- Technic forms into molded generic machinery.

Normal-map edge reinforcement may replace geometry on low-value distant parts.

# PART VIII — CONTROLLED GAMEPLAY SCALE ADAPTATION

Literal physical-set scale is subordinate to RTS readability.

The scale ladder uses the minifigure as a visual anchor.

| Class | Visual scale relationship |
|---|---|
| Minifigure / worker | 1.0 reference |
| Tiny scout | ~1.5–2.2 minifigure heights |
| Small machine | ~2–3 |
| Medium vehicle | ~3–4.5 |
| Heavy machine | ~4.5–6 |
| Massive/Huge machine | ~6–10 |
| Normal structure | reads as a facility, not a set display |
| Command/major structure | dominant base landmark |

Very small official designs may be enlarged by approximately **15–35%** relative to literal scale.

The largest source machines may be compressed by approximately **10–20%** if necessary to preserve medium-scale battle readability.

Structure adaptation may be larger because buildings must function as RTS facilities.

Gameplay footprints remain authoritative.

Scale adaptation never changes the order of source hierarchy.

A Chrome Crusher must still feel larger than a Loader Dozer.

A Mothership must still feel extraordinary.

# PART IX — MINIFIGURE TREATMENT

Characters remain unmistakably minifigure-derived.

Canonical anatomy:

- cylindrical head;
- standard torso proportions;
- rigid upper/lower arm geometry;
- C-shaped hands;
- minifigure hips;
- rigid legs;
- source-faithful helmets and equipment.

There are:

- no realistic elbows;
- no deforming knees;
- no human finger articulation;
- no realistic facial anatomy.

Animation gains expressiveness through:

- shoulder rotation;
- wrist/tool orientation;
- hip rotation;
- rigid leg swings;
- torso lean;
- head tracking;
- whole-body compression;
- timing;
- staging.

Feet may make minor root-corrected translations to prevent skating.

No limb bends through geometry that a minifigure does not possess.

# PART X — VEHICLE SCALE

## Tiny scouts

Slightly enlarged relative to minifigures so their locomotion silhouette remains clear.

## Medium vehicles

Most faithful to source proportions.

## Heavy industrial machinery

Tools may receive controlled enlargement.

The body should not simply scale upward equally.

## True-air craft

Wingspan and altitude separation are emphasized enough that they remain distinct from hover.

## Massive machines

Compression primarily affects empty hull length and repetitive geometry.

Hero mechanisms remain large.

Huge assets may occupy significant screen space at close zoom but must leave readable combat frontage at 44 cells.

# PART XI — STRUCTURE SCALE

Structures use **architectural adaptation**, not literal set-display scale.

A building must read as:

- somewhere machinery could be produced;
- somewhere workers could operate;
- a legitimate economic or technological facility.

Recognizable source modules may be:

- repeated;
- widened;
- mirrored;
- connected through new compatible LEGO-like construction.

Major source mechanisms remain recognizable.

For example:

- Aero Tube Hangar retains pump/tube infrastructure;
- Eagle Command Base retains modular Mars Mission command architecture;
- Rock Raiders HQ retains industrial modularity and crane/worksite language.

Late-game base silhouettes remain:

**Rock Raiders:** dense worksite.

**Astronauts:** organized expedition campus.

**Aliens:** compact deployed craft complex.

**Martians:** distributed settlement network.

No late-game faction may converge into the same “generic RTS city.”

# PART XIII — SILHOUETTE REVIEW

Every unit must pass four gates.

## BLACK SILHOUETTE TEST

Render model black against mid-gray.

Role/source family should remain identifiable.

## 44-CELL TEST

Render actual normal combat camera.

The unit must be distinguishable among at least six other friendly types.

## MIXED-ARMY TEST

Test partial occlusion, turning, retreat and combat.

A distinctive feature must remain visible from common angles.

## MIRROR TEST

Add team identification to two identical factions.

Ownership must read without recoloring canonical bodies.

Failure at any gate requires silhouette improvement before additional texture detail.

# PART XVI — LOD PHILOSOPHY

Three gameplay LOD families are canonical.

## CLOSE LOD

Preserves production-level LEGO construction and visible operators.

## COMBAT LOD

Primary production target.

Simplifies hidden/internal brickwork while preserving every Primary and most Secondary feature.

## STRATEGIC LOD

Preserves:

- outer silhouette;
- cockpit/canopy mass;
- main drill/tool;
- major wheels/tracks;
- major transparent elements;
- large faction-color blocks;
- deployed/transformed silhouette.

Simplifies:

- underside studs;
- internal clips;
- decorative bars;
- tiny hoses;
- hidden seat controls.

Crossfade/dither transition is preferred where geometry changes are large.

Visible silhouette popping during zoom is unacceptable.

# PART XVIII — TEAM COLOR SYSTEM

The canonical ownership system uses:

# IDENTIFICATION TILES + SIGNAL BAND + SELECTION LANGUAGE.

Every buildable vehicle/structure includes one or more small model-integrated **Identification Tiles** occupying roughly 2–5% of visible exterior area.

These appear as:

- removable upper panel;
- antenna band;
- docking marker;
- machinery flag plate;
- small light housing.

Their precise physical form follows faction construction language.

Team identification is reinforced by:

- selection ring;
- health-bar identity pip;
- minimap symbol;
- target brackets.

The body palette does not change.

Mirror readability therefore comes from location and graphic language, not repainting.

# PART XX — CRYSTAL VISUAL LANGUAGE

Crystals are one global strategic resource.

## World deposit

Clustered faceted formations with a bright translucent green-family interior and pale luminous edge.

Geometry is globally consistent across biomes.

## Carried Crystal

One visually recognizable faceted Crystal held or mechanically clamped.

Scale is slightly enlarged for readability.

## Stored Crystal

Same Crystal inside faction-specific containment.

## Rock Raiders

Mechanical clamps, crates and illuminated industrial racks.

## Astronauts

Clean containment cylinder or extraction cassette.

## Aliens

Crystal remains physically recognizable inside black/lime resonance housings.

Committed Crystal gains an enclosing mechanical ring; the Crystal itself does not mutate.

## Martians

Crystal sits in eccentric clamps, tube-connected handling machinery or articulated racks.

## Crystal Reaper

Harvesting physically grips/cuts/extracts Crystal formations rather than making them vanish into a beam.

## Resonance Core

Several recognizably standard Crystals are mechanically committed around a central ETX resonance apparatus.

The resource does not become a separate “Alien crystal.”

# PART XXI — ORE / METAL

Raw Ore uses:

- warm brown;
- rust;
- ochre;
- dark mineral inclusions;
- restrained metallic flecks.

It appears as:

- geological seams;
- fractured boulders;
- loose chunks;
- mined cargo.

- gold coins;
- fantasy treasure;

- cool gray LEGO-like industrial ingots/modules;

- faction-specific containers.

- boulder count;
- seam volume;
- exposed mineral saturation.

An exhausted deposit remains readable as worked geology.

# PART XXII — ENERGY

Energy is visible through operating infrastructure.

It is never represented as a floating world pickup.

## Generation

Communicated through moving machinery:

- Raider generators;
- Astronaut solar tracking;
- Alien couplers;
- Martian pumps/pressure machinery.

## Reserve

Shown primarily in HUD and local status panels.

## Demand

High-demand buildings display faster/stronger operational cycles.

## Brownout

World cues:

- machinery slows/stops;
- status lamps pulse in a low-power pattern;
- nonessential emissives extinguish;
- defense mechanisms park;
- no universal darkness filter.

The UI reinforces brownout with a power-break icon and striped state treatment.

# PART XXV — SHADOWS

Three-level system:

## Directional shadow

Large soft shadow mass communicates:

- grounding;
- vehicle scale;
- cliff height;
- aircraft elevation.

## Contact shadow

Short-range ambient/contact darkening connects wheels, tracks and feet to terrain.

## Micro shadow

Tiny brick/stud self-shadowing is reduced aggressively at normal/far zoom.

No dense field of tiny stud shadows may create visual noise.

# PART XXVI — TRUE-AIR READABILITY

True-air units communicate altitude through:

- constant vertical separation;
- displaced ground shadow;
- slightly faster parallax;
- air-layer selection anchor;
- subtle downward marker only when selected.

Ground hover remains close enough to the terrain for its shadow/body relationship to read as a surface unit.

Tall walkers remain physically grounded through foot contact and vertical leg motion.

No fake altitude control exists.

# PART XXVII — ENVIRONMENT VS PLAYABLE ASSETS

Environment targets approximately:

- 10–20% less local saturation than playable faction body colors;
- lower high-frequency detail inside combat lanes;
- fewer emissive elements;
- less constant motion.

Motion priority:

1. playable unit;
2. projectile/hazard;
3. resource;
4. environmental decoration.

Permanent outlines are not a default readability tool.

# PART XXVIII — ENVIRONMENTAL LEGO LANGUAGE

The canonical world is a hybrid.

Large landforms use:

- broad stylized faceting;
- stepped geological forms;
- chunky readable strata;

- modular fractured plates;
- boulder forms;

Broad rough faceted surfaces; minimal micro-noise.

## Sand

Clearly modular LEGO-like plate systems without showing a permanent gameplay grid.

Large geological forms with low-frequency mineral variation.

# PART XXXI — ROUGH GROUND

Rough Ground is identified through geometry rather than tint.

Cues include:

- rubble density;
- fractured tiles;
- broken ice;
- angular fragments;
- interrupted wheel-scale surface rhythm;
- visible small vertical relief.

It remains readable under different lighting conditions.

---

# PART XXXII — SHALLOW / DEEP LIQUID

## Shallow

- visible bottom;
- lighter value;
- shoreline sediment;
- smaller wave rhythm;
- frequent protruding rocks/plates.

## Deep

- darker value;
- absent visible bottom;
- slower larger surface movement;
- stronger shoreline drop;
- deeper edge shadow.

The distinction remains visible without an overlay.

Water remains stylized, not photoreal simulated ocean rendering.

---

# PART XXXIII — ELEVATION

High/low ground uses:

- strong cliff profile;
- clear vertical face;
- top-edge shadow/value break;
- plateau surface change;
- readable ramps.

Broad gameplay elevations should remain understandable even in still screenshots.

Subtle naturalistic hills never replace strategically important level changes.

---

# PART XXXIV — RAMPS

All traversable strategic ramps use a consistent grammar:

- broad continuous floor;
- repeated lateral edge formations;
- no impassable rubble across the center;
- softened elevation transition;
- visible connection to both levels.

Decorative slopes use:

- broken edges;
- discontinuous surfaces;
- obstacles;
- steeper profiles.

---

# PART XXXV — EXCAVATABLE TERRAIN

Excavatable features appear naturally geological but deliberately suspicious.

Core cues:

- fracture line separating one rock mass from another;
- slightly different stratum orientation;
- embedded loose mineral fragments;
- shallow recess;
- repeating drill-access geometry.

No glowing “break me” outline is permanent.

When an eligible excavator is selected:

- fracture edges gain a restrained industrial dashed highlight;
- drill approach points appear;
- eligibility pattern is projected directly onto the rock face.

Progress evolves physically:

1. intact fracture;
2. chipped face;
3. exposed internal break;
4. partial opening;
5. final rubble clearing;
6. usable passage.

---

# PART XXXVI — HAZARDS

World behavior carries the first warning.

Phase 06 timings remain authoritative.

## Lava / volcanic vent

- glow increases;
- nearby cracks brighten;
- pressure plume appears;
- 2.5s buildup.

## Crystal discharge

- crystals pulse in synchronized waves;
- small arcs converge;
- local ground glows;
- 1.5s buildup.

## Rockfall

- dust drops first;
- small fragments fall;
- larger surface vibrates;
- 2s warning.

## Cryogenic vent

- frost plume contracts before burst;
- surface whiteness spreads;
- 1.5s buildup.

## Ice fracture

- visible branching crack propagation;
- approximately 3s readable spread.

## Unstable machinery

- repeating physical misalignment;
- warning lamps;
