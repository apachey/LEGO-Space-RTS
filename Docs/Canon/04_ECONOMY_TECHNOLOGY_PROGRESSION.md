# PART I — GLOBAL ECONOMY MODEL

## 1. MATCH ECONOMY LOOP

A normal competitive match follows a common economic rhythm while allowing substantially different faction execution.

### OPENING — approximately 0:00–3:30

The player begins with:

- one faction command structure;
- six workers;
- a small Ore reserve;
- a pre-charged Energy reserve;
- enough Operations Capacity to establish the first production infrastructure;
- no starting Crystals.

The first strategic decisions are normally:

- worker growth versus immediate production;
- scouting;
- first Energy investment;
- first combat capability;
- whether to prepare an early expansion.

Starting bases contain enough Ore to support meaningful opening variation but not enough to support a safe one-base late game.

No faction spends its opening merely turning on its economy.

### EARLY GAME — approximately 3:00–6:30

Players normally establish:

- one or two production facilities;
- dedicated Energy generation;
- scouting coverage;
- early defensive capability;
- a larger worker count.

Early conflict focuses on:

- workers;
- exposed production;
- future expansion sites;
- Energy structures;
- scouting denial.

Crystals begin to matter near the transition out of this period rather than from the first seconds of the match.

### FIRST EXPANSION — normally approximately 4:30–7:00

A first expansion is economically attractive before the starting Ore field is exhausted.

The timing differs by faction:

- Aliens can establish the cheapest operational foothold;
- Astronauts make a new outpost productive quickly;
- Martians create an immediately functional local settlement and then invest in connection;
- Rock Raiders make the largest maturation investment but achieve the strongest developed throughput.

No faction simultaneously receives the fastest expansion, strongest mature economy, and strongest expansion defense.

### MID-GAME — approximately 6:00–11:00

The defining faction economies become fully visible.

Rock Raiders build serviced industrial territory.

Astronauts begin meaningful refitting and parallel Field/Mission specialization.

Aliens commit Crystals and generate meaningful Charge.

Martians connect multiple settlement nodes.

Crystals are now a regular strategic concern.

Energy infrastructure becomes a meaningful harassment target rather than an opening formality.

### SECONDARY TECHNOLOGY — approximately 9:00–14:00

Players invest in:

- faction-system upgrades;
- heavier units;
- specialized economy;
- air transitions;
- siege;
- logistics.

This period is where technology choices begin producing major scouting information.

### ADVANCED GAME — approximately 12:00–18:00

The largest machinery and most sophisticated faction systems become available.

Advanced technology requires:

- substantial Ore;
- sustained Energy;
- Crystal expenditure;
- opportunity cost against additional army production or expansion.

Advanced technology therefore represents strategic escalation rather than an automatic timer.

### RESOURCE-PRESSURED LATE GAME — approximately 18:00 onward

Starting deposits are usually exhausted.

Natural expansions are increasingly depleted.

Players must contest:

- remaining rich Ore;
- late Crystal fields;
- exposed expansion locations;
- central or peripheral deep Ore seams.

Compositions become more resource-sensitive.

Players increasingly decide whether replacing a lost advanced machine is worth more than:

- several cheaper units;
- another expansion;
- rebuilding Energy infrastructure;
- preserving remaining Crystals.

# 2. RESOURCE NODE MODEL

## ORE / METAL

Ore appears physically as:

- exposed seams;
- boulder clusters;
- fractured mineral shelves;
- shallow extractable deposits;
- larger deep deposits on contested territory.

Ore always depletes.

### Prototype deposit sizes

| Deposit type | Approximate total Ore |
|---|---:|
| Small | 600 |
| Standard | 900 |
| Rich | 1,350 |
| Deep contested seam | 2,400 |

A normal starting position should contain approximately **1,800 safe Ore** distributed across multiple visible deposits.

Ore deposits visibly change as harvested:

- loose material disappears;
- the exposed face becomes smaller;
- industrial marks appear;
- the final exhausted state becomes visually obvious.

Richness is readable by deposit size and geological presentation rather than hidden yield multipliers.

---

## ENERGY — CANONICAL GLOBAL MODEL

Energy is **primarily generated through structures**.

There are no normal harvestable Energy crystals, Energy gas fields, or second mineral-node family.

This is the canonical global solution.

Energy therefore creates infrastructure decisions instead of functioning as Ore with another icon.

Energy exists in two simultaneous forms:

### ENERGY GENERATION

Measured as **Energy per second — E/s**.

Generators continuously produce Energy.

### ENERGY RESERVE

Surplus generation charges a stored Energy reserve.

One-time Energy expenditures consume this reserve.

Energy reserve supports:

- construction;
- production;
- research;
- refitting;
- excavation;
- emergency operation during temporary generator loss.

### CONTINUOUS ENERGY DEMAND

Some buildings and faction systems require continuous Energy.

Examples include:

- advanced production;
- defenses;
- Resonance Cores;
- Aero Tube Links;
- service systems.

Energy therefore asks two different questions:

**Can I afford this activation or construction now?**

and:

**Can my infrastructure continue operating it?**

---

## CRYSTALS

Crystals occur in visibly distinct clusters.

They are much scarcer than Ore.

### Prototype Crystal deposits

| Deposit type | Crystal yield |
|---|---:|
| Small | 5 |
| Standard | 8 |
| Rich | 14 |
| High-grade contested field | 20 |

Starting bases contain **no normal Crystal field**.

The first practical Crystal access normally lies:

- near a first expansion;
- between expansion routes;
- or on exposed contested territory.

Crystals visibly disappear one major piece at a time.

High-grade fields use:

- larger crystal formations;
- denser glowing clusters;
- stronger environmental illumination.

They are readable before harvesting begins.

# 3. BASE HARVESTING VALUES

Basic worker extraction uses common underlying rates so faction economy does not depend on hidden percentage modifiers.

### Basic Ore extraction

A basic worker extracts approximately:

**1 Ore every 1.5 seconds while actively mining.**

Travel, hauling, processing, and infrastructure determine effective income.

### Basic Crystal extraction

A basic worker extracts approximately:

**1 Crystal every 10 seconds while actively harvesting a Crystal deposit.**

Again, logistics determine actual delivered income.

### Carry capacity

Typical basic worker payload:

- 8 Ore;
- or 1 Crystal.

Specialized machinery can exceed these values.

# 4. RESOURCE OWNERSHIP & STORAGE

## Rock Raiders

Resources exist in three stages:

**raw → hauled → processed.**

Raw material remains local to its Worksite.

Processed resources can be shared across connected Worksites.

Disconnected Worksities retain their local processed reserve but cannot freely draw from another disconnected Worksite.

## Astronauts

Delivered resources enter a standardized faction reserve.

Astronaut resource banking is intentionally the easiest to understand.

Remote Mobile Mining Platforms still physically transport their payload to a receiving structure; matter is not teleported.

## Aliens

ETX Servitors return material to an ETX Command Core.

Once received, normal Ore and uncommitted Crystals enter the Alien faction reserve.

Alien economic complexity is concentrated in Crystal commitment rather than cargo accounting.

## Martians

Resources remain physically associated with the settlement at which they entered the economy.

Connected Aero Tube components expose these reserves as one pooled spendable network.

If the network is cut, each settlement retains the resources physically stored there.

This makes network disruption meaningful without destroying economic ownership.

---

# 5. RESOURCE DEPLETION & MAP PRESSURE

Safe starting resources are finite.

The intended strategic progression is:

**main base → first expansion → contested secondary expansions → remaining late-game deposits.**

Maps must not provide:

- effectively infinite safe starting Ore;
- infinitely regenerating Crystals;
- completely exhausted maps before advanced play can occur.

Late maps should contain a small number of exposed **Deep Ore Seams**.

These are still Ore.

They are not a fourth resource.

Their function is to prevent late matches from becoming economically inert while ensuring that the remaining economy exists in strategically dangerous territory.

---

# 6. ECONOMIC INFORMATION

Players never receive the opponent's exact banked-resource number merely by scouting.

Visible economic tells include:

- number of active expansions;
- approximate worker count;
- depleted resource geometry;
- processing infrastructure;
- generator count;
- advanced production structures;
- faction-system investment.

Faction-specific tells are defined later in this document.

Destroyed or previously scouted structures remain only as last-known fog information.

# PART II — GLOBAL CONSTRUCTION ECONOMY

## 1. COST RESERVATION

When a building order is placed:

1. its full cost becomes **reserved**;
2. the resource is unavailable for other spending;
3. if construction never begins, cancellation returns the reservation in full.

When physical construction begins:

- 20% of Ore and Energy cost becomes committed;
- the remaining 80% is consumed progressively during construction.

Crystal construction costs, where present, are committed at approximately 50% construction progress.

This prevents accidental placement from being excessively punitive while preserving genuine economic commitment.

---

## 2. CANCELLATION

After construction has begun:

- all unspent reserved resources are returned;
- 50% of already consumed Ore and Energy is returned;
- Crystals not yet consumed are returned completely;
- already consumed Crystals are not refunded.

A nearly complete structure is therefore meaningfully expensive to cancel.

---

## 3. BUILDING DESTRUCTION

Destroyed completed structures provide no automatic insurance refund.

Economic recovery comes from:

- surviving workers;
- remaining infrastructure;
- reserve resources;
- faction recovery systems;
- map control.

There is no hidden comeback reimbursement.

---

# 4. STRUCTURE REPAIR COST

Restoring a completely ruined-but-not-destroyed structure from nearly zero health to full condition would consume approximately:

- **30% of its original Ore cost**;
- **15% of its original one-time Energy cost**;
- no additional Crystals.

Actual repair cost scales with missing structural condition.

Final repair rates belong to the Combat & Balance phase.

---

# 5. UNIT REPAIR COST

At a dedicated repair/service facility:

- approximately 25–30% of original Ore value for a complete restoration;
- approximately 10% of original Energy production cost;
- no Crystal replacement cost for ordinary damage.

Field repairs are materially less resource-efficient.

Destroyed advanced machines must be rebuilt normally.

This prevents strong repair factions from making attrition irrelevant.

# PART III — OPERATIONS CAPACITY

The universal population-equivalent is:

# OPERATIONS CAPACITY — OC

Operations Capacity represents the amount of active personnel, vehicles, aircraft, machine crews, logistics support, command attention, and maintenance capacity the civilization can sustain.

It is intentionally broader than “population.”

---

## 1. GLOBAL RULES

- Workers consume OC.
- Mobile units consume OC.
- Larger machines consume more.
- Buildings do **not** consume OC.
- Buildings which genuinely expand operational infrastructure provide OC.
- There is no dedicated universal “supply building.”
- Standard competitive maximum: **100 OC**.
- Existing units suffer no penalty at cap.
- New units cannot begin fabrication if completing them would exceed the cap.

The UI gives warning at approximately 85% usage.

No combat debuff occurs for reaching the cap.

# PART IV — GLOBAL ENERGY MODEL

## 1. ENERGY DOMAINS

Energy is not managed through manually drawn electrical cables.

Structures instead belong to a faction-specific **Energy Domain**.

A domain contains:

- generation;
- stored reserve;
- continuous demand.

The four factions form domains differently.

### Rock Raiders

Connected Worksite components form one Energy Domain.

### Astronauts

Eagle Command Bases, Service & Refit Hubs, and Solar Energy Arrays automatically establish a standardized expedition power network over generous operating areas.

No manual cable placement exists.

### Aliens

Structures belonging to an operational ETX Command Core share its Energy Domain.

Alien bases are compact enough that separate cable topology is unnecessary.

### Martians

Every connected Aero Tube settlement component is an Energy Domain.

Disconnected settlements fall back to their local generation and reserve.

---

# 2. ENERGY RESERVE

Every primary command structure provides:

- **150 Energy reserve capacity**;
- **+2 E/s auxiliary generation**.

Dedicated generators increase both output and reserve.

The command generator is enough for opening activity.

It is not enough for a developed base.

---

# 3. SURPLUS ENERGY

If:

**generation > continuous demand**

surplus Energy charges the reserve.

If the reserve is full, additional surplus is wasted.

---

# 4. DEFICIT ENERGY

If:

**demand > generation**

the reserve automatically supplies the deficit.

This allows a base to survive:

- a generator being destroyed;
- temporary production spikes;
- short infrastructure interruptions.

---

# 5. BROWNOUT

When:

- demand exceeds generation;
- and reserve reaches zero;

the Energy Domain enters **brownout**.

Buildings are shut down in complete readable groups rather than rapidly flickering.

Default priority:

1. command and basic economic receiving;
2. resource processing;
3. production and research;
4. service systems and faction-special systems;
5. static defenses and nonessential infrastructure.

Players may mark an individual structure:

- High;
- Normal;
- Low;

priority.

Routine priority adjustment should rarely be necessary.

### Brownout effects

A disabled structure:

- stops production;
- pauses research;
- stops repair/refit;
- stops defensive operation;
- stops special faction-system generation where applicable.

Progress is retained.

Existing field units remain controllable.

Destroying one generator therefore does not instantly disable an entire army.

# PART V — ROCK RAIDER ECONOMY

# WORKSITE NETWORK

## 1. WHAT CREATES A WORKSITE

The following create Rock Raider service zones:

- Rock Raiders HQ;
- Vehicle Service Bay.

Prototype service radius:

- HQ — **18 build-grid cells**;
- Vehicle Service Bay — **12 build-grid cells**.

A Raider building is serviced when its operating center lies within one of these zones.

Two service zones belong to one Worksite component when their zones overlap.

No player-placed cable is required.

---

## 2. WHAT SERVICE PROVIDES

A serviced structure can:

- receive automatically routed raw resources;
- draw processed resources from the Worksite component;
- draw Energy from the component;
- use Worksite repair support;
- receive automated construction deliveries;
- participate fully in industrial production.

---

## 3. AUTOMATED HAULING

A serviced extraction site automatically generates non-selectable industrial hauling activity between:

- extraction;
- receiving;
- processing.

These logistics rigs are part of the Worksite system and not new buildable Phase 03 units.

The player manages:

- where mining occurs;
- where processors are built;
- Worksite geometry;
- whether routes are too long.

The player does **not** issue individual hauling orders.

---

## 4. DISCONNECTED BUILDINGS

A disconnected structure does not become inert immediately.

It retains:

- already reserved production materials;
- a short local Energy buffer;
- local worker support.

It loses:

- access to remote Worksite reserves;
- automatic long-distance hauling;
- shared Worksite Energy;
- Worksite repair support.

An isolated production building can therefore finish an already supplied queue but cannot operate indefinitely without reconnection.

---

# RESOURCE FLOW

The canonical Raider flow is:

# EXTRACT → HAUL → PROCESS

### Rock Raider Crew

Performs:

- basic Ore extraction;
- Crystal extraction;
- construction;
- repair.

Inside a serviced Worksite, Crew normally remains at the extraction face while logistics collects completed loads.

Outside service, Crew must physically carry material.

### Drill Craft

Excavates terrain.

It is not the normal Ore economy unit.

### Loader Dozer

Supports industrial work and rubble clearing but is not treated as a generic worker replacement.

---

# ORE

Ore is delivered to:

- Rock Raiders HQ for limited emergency receiving;
- Ore Processing Plant for full industrial processing.

HQ processing exists so a new Worksite has immediate economic value.

The Ore Processing Plant is required for mature throughput.

Rock Raider extraction advantage therefore comes from:

- keeping miners at the resource face;
- automatic hauling;
- multiple receiving lanes;
- strong processing;
- access to difficult geology.

It does not come from a hidden “+20% mining speed” faction passive.

---

# CRYSTALS

Rock Raider Crew can extract Crystals before a Crystal Vault exists.

Before a Crystal Vault is operational:

- HQ may hold up to 6 raw Crystals;
- they cannot be spent on advanced technology.

A Crystal Vault:

- processes raw Crystals;
- makes them spendable;
- protects advanced industrial handling;
- unlocks the advanced technology stage.

Crystals remain secondary to the Raider Ore economy.

---

# ENERGY

## Power Station

Prototype output:

- **+10 E/s**;
- **+120 Energy reserve capacity**.

Power is shared through the connected Worksite component.

Distant independent Worksites require local generation until physically integrated.

Major consumers include:

- Engineering Workshops;
- Cutter Masts;
- advanced repair;
- advanced production.

---

# EXPANSION ECONOMICS

A new Rock Raiders HQ is immediately useful because it provides:

- Crew production;
- basic receiving;
- service radius;
- Operations Capacity;
- auxiliary power.

It matures by adding:

1. Ore Processing Plant;
2. Power Station;
3. appropriate production/service.

The mature expansion is the most industrially capable expansion in the game.

It is also the most expensive to fully establish.

---

# REPAIR ECONOMY

Inside a serviced Worksite:

- Crew can access standardized repair materials;
- Vehicle Service Bays provide full machine servicing;
- repair is economically efficient.

Field repair beyond service costs materially more Ore and Energy.

Heavy machines are therefore excellent at sustained operations **when the Worksite advances with them**.

A Chrome Crusher abandoned deep beyond industrial support remains expensive to maintain.

---

# EXCAVATION ECONOMICS

Primary excavation unit:

- Drill Craft.

Later-capable units:

- Granite Grinder;
- Chrome Crusher.

Crew may clear light rubble but cannot open major authored rock features.

### Standard excavation feature

Approximate requirement:

- **25 Energy**;
- 15–35 seconds depending on machine and feature.

### Reinforced excavation feature

Approximate requirement:

- **50 Energy**;
- 30–60 seconds.

Excavation also occupies machinery that could otherwise be:

- mining;
- defending;
- attacking.

Opening terrain therefore creates a real opportunity cost.

# FIELD SYSTEMS VS MISSION SYSTEMS

The economy remains unified.

There are not two human resource pools.

## Field Systems spending profile

Generally emphasizes:

- Ore;
- moderate Energy;
- sustainment;
- remote operation;
- support infrastructure.

## Mission Systems spending profile

Generally emphasizes:

- larger Energy reserve costs;
- more Crystal expenditure;
- specialized production;
- aerospace;
- transformation.

A Field-heavy strategy therefore usually has more economic room for expansion.

A Mission-heavy strategy converts more Energy and Crystals into specialized capability.

Neither tradition replaces the other.

## ANTI-ABUSE RULE

After a successful Mission Refit, that asset has a:

**20-second Configuration Lock**

during which it cannot begin another refit.

This prevents instant perfect counter-switching.

---

## SERVICE-HUB DESTRUCTION DURING REFIT

If the servicing structure is destroyed:

- refit cancels;
- the asset returns to its previous configuration after emergency reassembly;
- 50% of the installation Ore/Energy cost is refunded;
- a previously owned module remains owned;
- a new Crystal-paid module that had passed 50% installation retains its spent Crystal cost.

The unit is not destroyed merely because the facility was destroyed.

**18 build-grid cells.**

Deployed Solar Explorer service radius:

**10 build-grid cells.**

Service coverage provides:

- repair;

Armies do not need to stand beside a Service Hub merely to fight normally.

# PART VII — ALIEN ECONOMY

# BASELINE ECONOMY

Basic Alien play functions without Crystal Charge.

ETX Servitors:

- harvest Ore;
- harvest Crystals;
- construct;
- repair structures.

Resources are delivered to ETX Command Cores.

Ore builds the physical technological civilization.

Energy operates it.

Crystals escalate it.

---

# POWER COUPLER

Prototype output:

- **+12 E/s**;
- **+100 Energy reserve capacity**.

Alien generators have the highest individual output because Resonance systems also create the highest continuous Energy pressure.

---

# RESONANCE CORES

## CONSTRUCTION RULE

A Resonance Core:

- requires an ETX Command Core;
- must belong to that Command Core's Energy Domain;
- is limited to **one Resonance Core per ETX Command Core**.

Every base does not automatically receive one.

The player must build it.

This makes an Alien Crystal commitment visible and attackable.

---

# CRYSTAL COMMITMENT

Baseline Core commitment slots:

**4 Crystals.**

After **Expanded Resonance Lattice**:

**6 Crystals.**

Commit action:

- one Crystal at a time;
- 8 seconds per Crystal.

A committed Crystal:

- leaves the spendable Crystal bank;
- increases Charge capacity;
- generates Charge;
- increases continuous Energy demand.

---

# WITHDRAWAL

Withdrawal takes:

**15 seconds per Crystal.**

At withdrawal start:

- that Crystal immediately stops generating Charge;
- its Charge capacity contribution is removed.

If current Charge is now above the new maximum, excess Charge is discharged.

At completion:

- the Crystal returns to the normal spendable reserve.

This prevents the player from banking a huge Charge reserve and instantly converting every Crystal back into technology currency.

---

# RESONANCE CORE ENERGY DEMAND

Each operational Resonance Core demands:

**3 E/s base**

plus:

**2 E/s for every committed Crystal.**

Examples:

| Committed Crystals | Core demand |
|---|---:|
| 0 | 3 E/s |
| 1 | 5 E/s |
| 2 | 7 E/s |
| 4 | 11 E/s |
| 6 | 15 E/s |

A serious Alien Charge economy therefore requires visible Power Coupler investment.

---

# CHARGE — FINAL FORMULA

Charge remains a derived faction resource.

It cannot be harvested on the map.

## Maximum Charge

For each operational Resonance Core:

**+20 Charge base capacity**

For each committed Crystal:

**+20 Charge capacity**

Therefore:

**Maximum Charge = 20 × operational Cores + 20 × committed Crystals**

Example:

One Core with four committed Crystals:

**100 maximum Charge.**

---

## CHARGE GENERATION

Each committed Crystal generates:

**0.4 Charge per second.**

Therefore:

| Committed Crystals | Generation |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 0.4/s |
| 2 | 0.8/s |
| 4 | 1.6/s |
| 6 | 2.4/s |

Energy does not increase Charge efficiency.

Energy is a prerequisite for the Core to operate.

If the Core browns out:

- generation pauses completely;
- committed Crystals remain committed.

This preserves the Crystal-versus-technology dilemma.

---

# CHARGE STORAGE

Charge does not naturally decay while below maximum.

At maximum:

- generation pauses;
- no overflow is created.

The player is therefore encouraged to spend Charge but is not forced into constant ability spam.

---

**50 Charge**

**18 seconds**

## 2. RAPID FABRICATION

Requires:

**Rapid Fabrication Conduits.**

ETX Fabricator injection:

- 25 Charge;
- removes up to 15 seconds from the current unit's remaining fabrication time.

Reconfiguration Dock injection:

- 35 Charge;
- removes up to 20 seconds.

One injection maximum per produced unit.

It can never reduce remaining fabrication below 10 seconds.

Production acceleration is therefore useful for timing but intentionally competes unfavorably with a well-executed combat Surge.

---

## 3. DEFENSE RESONANCE SHUNT

Requires:

**Defense Resonance Shunt.**

Cost:

**25 Charge**

Duration:

**12 seconds**

One ETX Defense Node temporarily enters an emergency high-output state.

This is intentionally a defensive emergency use rather than the most efficient normal Charge expenditure.

---

# RESONANCE CORE DESTRUCTION

When a Resonance Core is destroyed:

1. its Charge capacity disappears immediately;
2. Charge above the new maximum is discharged;
3. committed Crystals become unstable.

A destroyed Core leaves one visible:

# RESONANCE SALVAGE CACHE

Baseline intact Crystals:

**ceil(committed Crystals ÷ 2)**

The remainder are destroyed.

The cache:

- persists for 45 seconds;
- can be recovered by either faction;
- is claimed when a worker remains beside it for 4 uninterrupted seconds.

**Resonant Recovery Latches** add one additional intact Crystal to the cache, up to the number originally committed.

This creates economic danger without transferring the entire committed investment through one structure kill.

---

# SURGE WINDOW ECONOMY

A powerful Alien attack normally requires all four:

1. Crystal access;
2. Crystal commitment;
3. sufficient Power Couplers;
4. time to accumulate Charge.

Enemy scouting can detect:

- Resonance Core count;
- lit commitment arms;
- Power Coupler count;
- increasing Core illumination.

A major Alien attack is therefore economically telegraphed.

---

# PERMANENT TECHNOLOGY VS TEMPORARY POWER

Alien advanced research consumes permanent Crystals.

A Crystal spent on:

- ETX Reconfiguration;
- Expanded Resonance;
- Infiltration;
- Siege;
- Mothership technology;

cannot support Charge.

A Crystal committed to Charge remains reclaimable but unavailable while committed.

The player repeatedly chooses:

**future capability**

against:

**current operational intensity.**

No research improves Charge gained **per committed Crystal**.

This prevents the dilemma from disappearing in late game.

---

# ALIEN EXPANSION

The ETX Command Core is:

- cheaper than Raider HQ maturation;
- operational quickly;
- capable of basic harvesting immediately.

A Crystal-focused expansion then requires:

- Power Coupler;
- Resonance Core;
- committed Crystals.

That turns a cheap foothold into a visible high-value target.

Alien expansions are therefore:

**fast to establish, expensive to intensify, comparatively vulnerable to sustained siege.**

# PART VIII — MARTIAN ECONOMY

# DISTRIBUTED SETTLEMENT ECONOMY

Every Martian settlement stores and operates locally.

A Settlement Station provides:

- local resource reserve;
- Operations Capacity;
- auxiliary Energy;
- worker production;
- Tube endpoint functionality.

A disconnected settlement is a real functioning base.

It does not become an inert island.

---

# LOCAL RESOURCE COLLECTION

Worker Robots deliver material to:

- Excavation Plant;
- Settlement Station;
- Aero Tube Hangar.

Baseline local storage:

### Aero Tube Hangar

- 500 Ore;
- 10 Crystals.

### Settlement Station

- 300 Ore;
- 6 Crystals.

### Excavation Plant

adds:

- 250 Ore;
- 8 Crystals

to its local settlement capacity.

Storage limits apply primarily to disconnected/local reserves.

A connected network presents the component's aggregate reserve in the UI.

---

# AERO TUBE NETWORK

## 1. STATIONS

Valid major Tube nodes:

- Aero Tube Hangar;
- Settlement Station.

---

## 2. LINK CONSTRUCTION

A Tube Link connects two eligible Stations.

The game automatically constructs a readable route between endpoints.

The player selects:

- origin;
- destination.

The player does not draw every bend.

---

## 3. LINK COST

Prototype cost:

**50 Ore base + 2 Ore per build-grid cell of link length.**

Typical practical link:

**70–110 Ore.**

One-time Energy activation:

**10 Energy.**

Construction time:

**10 seconds + 0.4 seconds per build-grid cell.**

---

## 4. LINK ENERGY DEMAND

Each active Tube Link demands:

**1 E/s.**

A destroyed or voluntarily disabled Link has no demand.

---

## 5. CONNECTION LIMIT

Baseline:

### Settlement Station

- 3 simultaneous Tube connections.

### Aero Tube Hangar

- 5 simultaneous Tube connections.

**Redundant Routing** increases each by one.

This prevents unreadable spaghetti networks while still allowing deliberate redundancy.

---

# RESOURCE DISTRIBUTION

Resources are not simulated as individual strategic cargo packets.

When two settlements are connected:

- their stored reserves become jointly spendable;
- spending automatically draws from the connected component.

Visible hypersled/cargo movement communicates this flow.

It does not require player routing.

There is no resource congestion mechanic.

This deliberately keeps Martian complexity focused on network topology.

---

# ENERGY DISTRIBUTION

Energy uses:

# LOCAL GENERATION + NETWORK BALANCING

Every connected Tube component pools:

- generation;
- reserve;
- continuous demand.

If the network breaks:

- the new components immediately separate;
- each retains its physically local generators;
- each retains its local Energy reserve.

Destroying one Pressure Generator therefore does not switch off the entire Martian civilization unless the player constructed a network with no redundancy and no other generation.

---

# PRESSURE GENERATOR

Prototype output:

- **+9 E/s**;
- **+100 Energy reserve capacity**.

---

# ELIGIBLE UNIT TRANSFER

Aero Tubes transfer only:

- Worker Robot;
- Double Hover;
- Jet Scooter.

They do **not** transfer:

- Aero Skiff;
- Red Planet Cruiser;
- Recon-Mech RP;
- Red Planet Protector;
- Excavation Searcher.

Heavy Martian units therefore still care about conventional map routes.

---

# UNIT TRANSFER TIME

Transfer sequence:

- 3 seconds loading;
- 0.12 seconds per Tube build-grid cell;
- 2 seconds unloading.

A typical practical transfer therefore lasts approximately:

**8–12 seconds.**

---

# STATION TRANSFER CAPACITY

Baseline:

- two simultaneous unit transfers per Station.

After **Hypersled Throughput**:

- three simultaneous transfers.

Additional transfers queue automatically.

There is no player cargo-routing screen.

---

# NETWORK DISRUPTION

If a Link breaks while a unit is in transit:

### Without Redundant Routing

The unit returns safely to its origin Station with a 6-second delay.

### With Redundant Routing

If another valid route exists, the transfer automatically reroutes.

Units are not arbitrarily killed by Tube destruction.

---

# EXPANSION ECONOMICS

A Settlement Station is immediately useful.

Before connection it provides:

- local worker production;
- local storage;
- local Energy reserve;
- local construction base.

After connection it gains:

- pooled resources;
- network Energy;
- reinforcement transfer;
- redundancy.

Martian expansion therefore follows:

# USEFUL LOCAL NODE → STRONGER CONNECTED NODE

# PART XXVII — SNOWBALL CONTROL

There is no artificial comeback bonus.

Snowball control emerges from six systemic principles.

## 1. EXPANSIONS ARE EXPOSED

Additional economy requires geographically vulnerable territory.

## 2. INFRASTRUCTURE HAS OPPORTUNITY COST

A player cannot simultaneously maximize:

- army;
- expansion;
- research;
- Energy;
- faction-system investment.

## 3. TECH COSTS REAL RESOURCES

Advanced research permanently removes Ore, Energy reserve, and often Crystals that could have built combat units.

## 4. SAFE RESOURCES DEPLETE

A player with a lead must continue moving outward.

## 5. FACTION POWER SYSTEMS CREATE TARGETS

- Raider service nodes;
- Astronaut Service Hubs;
- Alien Resonance Cores;
- Martian Links/Stations

all expose the mechanism behind faction advantage.

## 6. BUILDINGS DO NOT CAUSE INSTANT TOTAL COLLAPSE

Energy reserves, disconnected local functionality, and retained production progress prevent one small raid from automatically ending the game.

The winning player retains a real advantage.

The game simply continues to provide strategic ways to attack how that advantage is maintained.

# PART XXVIII — RESOURCE STARVATION & LATE GAME

## WHEN STARTING ORE DEPLETES

Workers move toward:

- expansions;
- exposed deep Ore seams;
- abandoned earlier sites.

A one-base player becomes economically unsustainable.

## WHEN NEARBY CRYSTALS ARE GONE

Players must decide whether remaining Crystal reserves belong in:

- advanced research;
- high-tier units;
- Alien commitment;
- preserved bank.

Crystal-heavy losses become increasingly consequential.

## WHEN ENERGY IS REPEATEDLY DESTROYED

Players can still:

- use stored reserve temporarily;
- rebuild generators;
- shut down low-priority systems;
- operate existing field armies.

Repeated Energy attacks progressively reduce:

- production;
- research;
- defensive operation;
- special faction systems.

## LATE-GAME ECONOMIC PATH

Phase 05 maps must retain at least some:

- dangerous remaining Ore;
- contested expansion territory.

The intended late game is not:

**“both sides have armies but no possible economy.”**

It is:

**“the remaining economy lies in increasingly dangerous territory.”**

# PART XXXV — CANON ESTABLISHED BY PHASE 04

The following decisions are now authoritative.

## GLOBAL ECONOMY

1. The game retains exactly three primary strategic resources:
   - Ore / Metal;
   - Energy;
   - Crystals.

2. Ore is a finite harvestable construction material.

3. Energy is primarily structure-generated rather than a second mineral node.

4. Energy has:
   - generation;
   - reserve;
   - continuous demand;
   - one-time expenditure.

5. Brownouts use automatic priority and reserve depletion rather than instantly disabling an entire base.

6. Crystals are finite scarce advanced-resource deposits concentrated around contested territory.

7. Starting positions normally contain Ore but no normal Crystal field.

8. Late maps retain exposed Deep Ore Seams using the existing Ore resource.

---

## OPERATIONS CAPACITY

9. The universal population system is **Operations Capacity**.

10. Workers and units consume OC.

11. Buildings do not consume OC.

12. Real command/production/service infrastructure increases OC.

13. There is no dedicated supply-tax building.

14. Standard maximum is 100 OC.

---

## ROCK RAIDERS

15. Rock Raider economy follows:

**extract → haul → process.**

16. Worksite hauling is automatic.

17. Rock Raiders HQ and Vehicle Service Bay create Worksite service zones.

18. Overlapping service zones form connected Worksite components.

19. Connected Worksites share:
   - processed resources;
   - Energy;
   - hauling;
   - repair/service support.

20. Disconnected infrastructure remains locally functional but loses network logistics.

21. Ore Processing Plant is the mature Ore-processing structure.

22. Crystal Vault converts raw Crystals into spendable advanced material.

23. Power Station provides 10 E/s baseline.

24. Raider economic advantage comes from infrastructure throughput rather than passive mining multipliers.

25. Excavation costs Energy and machine time.

26. Drill Craft is the primary excavation asset.

27. Mature Raider expansions are the most expensive to develop but possess the strongest local industrial throughput.

28. Industrial Expansion Program defines Mid progression.

29. Deep-Core Engineering defines Advanced progression.
