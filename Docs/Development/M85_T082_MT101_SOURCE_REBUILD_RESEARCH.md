# MT-101: source identity and finished appearance reconstruction

## Conclusion

The MT-101 proposal rejected after `54861b8` failed at the level of vehicle
identity, not merely the nested mini-bike. Its retained generic rover shape
became an inappropriate constraint on successive corrections. A valid internal
fit check of that shape could not make it resemble the official set.

The new proposal restarts from the official instruction cover, with construction
pages controlling specific components. Four recorded source-led generations
restore the main composition and correct three independently caught component
substitutions well enough for director appearance review. This is an
explicit self-assessment, not acceptance, exact LEGO reconstruction or a model
ready for gameplay integration. The previous proposal is director-rejected;
historical generation records and native artifacts are retained.

## Scope and evidence strength

LEGO identifies the source as set 7699, released in 2007, with 635 pieces and two
instruction books.[^catalog] Those books are the primary construction evidence;
their covers also provide the relevant original promotional image. The current
task concerns appearance of the assembled ground vehicle, including its nested
human spacecraft and bike. It does not authorize independent vehicle gameplay.

Source construction establishes that the ground chassis has its own permanent
front cabin. The rear spacecraft has a separate cockpit and carries a two-wheel
mini-bike. Book 1 pages 8 and 29 distinguish the bike from the spacecraft.[^book1]
Book 2 page 42 depicts the bike entering from the rear with the curved shells
opened, followed by closure; page 43 is a component separation illustration, not
proof of a literal upward insertion axis.[^book2]

The image of the assembled set on KC Brick Store's product page provides an
independent visual cross-check, not piece-by-piece certification. Its page
explicitly warns that pre-built sets are not guaranteed complete. It confirms
the distinctive open framework, cabin and wheel proportions; the drill is posed
downward there, unlike the forward-pointing cover pose.[^photo] Therefore pose
differences must not be mistaken for different vehicle designs.

## Recognition hierarchy

Identity should be assessed in this order: overall body mass and proportions,
component arrangement, distinctive original shapes, then small detail. The last
iteration inverted this order: improving a small bike was treated as sufficient
while the cabin, shields, wheels and drill still belonged to another vehicle.

The original's tall nose, curved middle body and visibly attached tail form
three contrasting masses. The new image retains that progression. Its dark
open chassis remains subordinate to the pale body shells and broad wheel
barrels. It reads as the unusually constructed Mars Mission vehicle rather than
a contemporary armored mining truck. This interpretation is supported by the
source photographs but remains visual judgement.

| Recognition feature | Rejected proposal's failure | New proposal inspection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front cabin | Low, broad generic armored-rover nose | Narrow steep closed canopy, pale angular borders, visible pilot |
| Middle body | Flat deck and generic fenders | Large curved pale studded shells with recessed side openings |
| Running gear | Ordinary-looking tire modules | Broad light-gray barrel wheels and pale open spoked hubs |
| Chassis | Solid vehicle slab dominated the body | Dark open beams and external articulated wheel links |
| Drilling tool | Familiar continuous helical auger | Black spiky tip and two separated pale star cutters |
| Tool support | Generic machinery/crane interpretation | Slender dark Technic-style boom and restrained hose |
| Weapon ownership | Correct labels did not guarantee recognizable geometry | Distinct upper sphere launcher, separate from drill |
| Rear module | Large exposed deck/craft broke main mass distribution | Tubular sides and adjustable black fins attached behind shells |

These are appearance checks, not automated geometric assertions. One raster can
show recognizable wheel construction without proving all six contacts. It can
show an attached rear craft without revealing the contained bike or proving
that the bike fits. The current image exposes three near-side wheels and one
far-side wheel; the other two contacts remain occluded.

## Drill and body observations that changed the interpretation

The primary source shows a black tapered toothed tip, two separated flower-like
cutting discs, a ribbed spacer and a rear shaft/collar. The distinct upper
launcher and slender articulated boom must stay separate. The white central
shells are curved, studded and hinged, not flat deck armor. Broad molded wheels,
external black links, ribbed struts and two upright dish headlights complete the
recognition cluster.[^book2]

Calling the tool an “auger” in previous appearance notes encouraged the generator
to substitute a standard spiral screw. That substitution seems mechanically
plausible, but plausibility is not fidelity. In the new prompt the original
spiky tip and two star cutters are explicit; a continuous helix is forbidden.
Likewise, “heavy chassis” is insufficient to preserve this vehicle: it readily
produces a slab-shaped tank unless the actual curved shells and open framework
remain the visual anchor.

The rear spacecraft's tubes must not be enlarged into generic jet engines.
Its compact source shape matters chiefly through how it sits inside the main
vehicle's composition. A separated view can demonstrate ownership later, but
placing a large craft beside the ground machine would obscure the requested
single assembled identity. The first new proposal therefore shows only the
assembled vehicle, rather than a fabricated extraction diagram.

## Proportion control and uncertainty

The parts catalog describes six giant hard-plastic wheels as 71 mm diameter and
47 mm width. This supplies a useful local width/diameter comparison of about
0.66, not a complete model measurement. The indexed inventory also distinguishes
two smaller bike wheels and the Bionicle drill component.[^inventory] Catalog
dimensions are secondary evidence; exact geometry should come from the actual
parts when production reconstruction begins.

For appearance assessment, dimensionless proportions are safer than converting
a perspective photograph directly to centimeters. The visible wheel barrel
width, wheel spacing, canopy steepness, body-shell height and tail size can be
compared with the cover at similar camera angles. Perspective foreshortening
means those comparisons are qualitative here. No measured deviation tolerance
or source-perfect dimension claim is recorded.

The generated wheel grooves and small decals are approximations. The image is
not a certified build instruction, parts inventory, six-wheel mesh, collision
model or transformation animation. The rear bike is hidden. In particular,
absence of an external oversized bike is not evidence that a correct miniature
bike exists inside the raster. Its required nesting remains established by the
source and the existing ownership contract, not by this view.

## Self-review and selection

The new image was checked first against the promotional cover for overall
identity, then against the permanent cabin/body construction, drill construction
and complete assembled nesting page. The independent assembled photo was used
to check whether the cover's dramatic pose had misled the body interpretation.
Finally, the image was reviewed without relying on its filename or labels: its
cabin, shields, wheel barrels and drill now carry recognition on their own.

The first reconstruction restored the body but enlarged the launcher into a
ring and obscured the rear spacecraft. Rev2 improved the tail but mutated the
launcher into a fin. Rev3 restored the compact Bionicle Zamor launcher with its
sphere, while independent review still found a cylindrical rear cockpit and the
drill's missing rear cup/collar. Rev4 corrects those last two areas without
changing the retained source-recognizable body. All four outputs and prompts are recorded, including
the three rejection findings. The director's permission for unlimited image
retries does not override gameplay or architecture stop rules or permit
self-acceptance.

The final selected artifact is
`ArtSource/M85/Preproduction/MT101SourceRebuildV1/mt101_source_rebuild_rev4.png`.
The complete prompt sequence is saved alongside it; built-in imagegen
was used. The generation manifest records five official reference roles and
their hashes, plus the complete per-attempt edit chain. Rev1 used the five
official references directly. Rev2–rev4 used the preceding source-rebuild
raster as a localized edit canvas together with the relevant official controls.
The rejected pre-rebuild generic rover generations and native blockouts never
served as appearance inputs; the rejected source-rebuild revisions remain
recorded because each became the next localized edit target.

## Prevention and boundaries

Future corrections of this asset must not preserve the rejected overall shape
merely because it has already been audited. Construction validity, source
resemblance, director acceptance and gameplay-camera readability are separate
claims. File-level validation now preserves rejection evidence, every
per-attempt input and hash, official control ownership, history and honest
occlusion limits; it cannot judge visual
quality itself.

The old native sampled upward extraction result remains historical. It is not
silently rewritten or promoted into evidence of the official rear-loading
mechanism. Rebuilding production geometry or changing the actual movement of
modules will require a separately scoped step after appearance review.

No canon, unit roles, costs, runtime models or simulation systems changed. T082
still requires renewed complete corpus/game-camera acceptance before the next
production gate. This new source-led image is a finished appearance proposal
ready for human resemblance judgement, not a claim that the entire milestone
has passed.
